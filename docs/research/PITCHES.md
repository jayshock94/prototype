# Glizzy Lovers — concept pitches and judge panel

Generated 2026-09-30 from the concept panel run. Research principles and citation verification are in `PRINCIPLES.md`; the recommendation is in `../CONCEPT.md`.

## F. The six concept pitches


### Pitch 1: Glizzy Lovers: Backyard Siege

*One grill. Four friends. Way too many raccoons.*

Glizzy Lovers is a mobile-first browser co-op game for one to four friends who host a neighborhood cookout while raccoons raid it in escalating, increasingly ridiculous waves. Everyone sees the whole backyard on one portrait screen: grill and picnic tables at the bottom, lawn in the middle, treeline at the top where the raccoons come from. You cook and throw glizzies to hungry guests, lay condiment defenses on the grass, bonk raccoons before they escape with the food or your hat, and ride the club's unicorns to haul saddlebags of dogs and charge through raids. It has Plants vs Zombies' wave curve and between-wave shop and Overcooked's hand-offs and shouting, but failure is a stolen hat, a faceplant or a slightly ruined party, never a game over that costs you anything. Join by a four-letter code as a guest in under ten seconds; make an account later only if you want your outfits on another phone.

- **Core loop.** Seconds: Left thumb moves, right thumb does one thing: DO. Tap DO to grab whatever is under you (raw dog from the cooler, cooked dog off the grill, a bun, a mustard bottle, a dropped hat), to place a topping defense on a lawn tile, or to bonk a raccoon if your hands are empty. Hold DO to aim (auto-magnetized to the nearest legal target: hungry guest, teammate, raccoon; drag to switch) and release to throw. Stand at the grill holding a raw dog and it cooks on a 4 s sizzle bar; leave it 4 s longer and it burns and smokes, and smoke attracts raccoons. Every action gets a 60 ms hit-stop, a particle, a sound and a 20 ms navigator.vibrate on Android. Near a unicorn post a RIDE button appears; mounted you move 2.2x faster, carry 4 glizzies in saddlebags instead of 1 in hand, and hold DO to charge, which knocks back everything in a line, teammates included. The unicorn has momentum and drift, spooks at smoke, and throws you if you hit the fence.
- Minutes: A wave is 45-60 s. A banner previews it ('Wave 3: two Hat Thieves'), raccoons pour down the treeline lanes toward the grill, serving table, guests' plates and players' hands, grab, and run back up their lane with the loot. The crew cooks, serves guests (throw a glizzy at a guest, they catch it, the shared Vibe meter rises) and bonks raccoons before they cross the top edge; a bonked raccoon drops the loot and ragdolls. A raccoon swarm can dogpile a player for 3 s and only a teammate can bonk them off. Wave end: a 5 s card shows team totals, each player's contribution as equal-sized icons (cooked, served, bonked, assists, hats recovered) and one blooper stat credited by name ('Grill fires: 2, both Priya'). Every 4th wave is a 30 s Topping Shop where the team spends pooled in-run Tips on placeables (mustard turret, relish slick, kraut fence raccoons route around, onion-ring tripwire, spicy dogs that make guests breathe fire), then a mini-boss wave.
- Session: One Cookout = 3 courses (Appetizers, Mains, Dessert), each 4 waves plus a mini-boss, 13-16 minutes total, ending in the Bandit King's Big Glizzy heist. Quick Snack = one course, 4-5 minutes, for a bus ride. If the Vibe meter empties the course ends with a 'Cookout slightly ruined' screen where the raccoons hold their own party on the picnic table; the crew keeps courses already cleared and retries instantly, no lives, no timers, nothing lost. The results card is a shareable image: crew name (derived from the room code), avatars in their outfits, waves cleared, funniest stat. 'One more?' keeps the room and the code.
- Meta: Buns (meta currency) are awarded per cookout from waves cleared, the same amount to every player, no RNG and no per-player ranking. Spent at the Merch Tent on cosmetics with fixed visible prices: hats, face gear, tops, backs, unicorn skins, saddles, emotes, throw trails. The Raccoon Field Guide sticker book fills deterministically (bonk each type N times, N shown); the Recipe Book tracks toppings tried. Both start partially filled: one hat from the creator, 2/16 stickers after the first course. New avatar slots unlock across sessions (back slot after cookout 2, throw trail after cookout 5). Optional 3 daily shift goals with no streaks and nothing lost for skipping. Growth comes from 3-4 named drops a year (new arena + raccoon type + cosmetic set), not a live service or a battle pass in v1.
- **Players / length:** 1-4. Works at 2 (raccoon count, spawn rate and course quota scale with headcount), sings at 4 (grill, runner, two riders). Solo gets NPC Uncle Ray on the grill. / Wave 45-60 s; course 4-5 min including one 30 s shop; full cookout 13-16 min; Quick Snack (one course) 4-5 min; Endless Cookout after the base cookout is beaten.
- **Room flow:** Tap Host: a 4-letter code from an alphabet without 0/O/1/I plus a share link (Colyseus custom-room-id recipe, room marked private). Tap Join: enter code and a display name (blank gets a food pun like 'Frank Furter'), in the lobby in under 10 s, no account, no reading. Lobby shows every avatar in its outfit, host picks arena and mode, ready checks, start. Drop-in mid-cookout: the newcomer trots in on a unicorn from the top edge at the next wave. Drop-out: seat held 60 s via allowReconnection, the avatar stands idle, and if a Yoinker steals the idle player's hat it is returned on rejoin. One code works across desktop and phone browsers, and the same room state renders both layouts.
- **Solo play:** Same rules on a narrower 5-lane arena. Uncle Ray cooks slowly and burns one dog in five, so the solo player runs, defends and occasionally rescues the grill. Solo runs the isomorphic sim module locally with no server room, so it works offline and never consumes server capacity. The solo first course is tuned so it cannot be lost. If solo playtests flat, the fallback is to rename it 'Practice Grill' and market the game as 2-4.
- **Touch controls:** Portrait, for three reasons: the arena is a lane-defense field (raccoons descend from the treeline at the top to the grill at the bottom), so tall is the natural shape; phones are held one-handed in portrait on a couch; and iOS cannot lock landscape anyway. Layout: a 10 x 16 tile playfield sized with 100svh and safe-area insets, HUD in the top 12% (Vibe meter, wave banner, timer) and controls in the bottom 18%. Left thumb: a floating joystick that spawns where the thumb lands anywhere in the lower-left 55% of the screen, with a size slider and an 8% dead zone. Right thumb: one 22%-width DO button (tap = grab/bonk/place, hold-and-release = throw with auto-aim, drag while holding to switch target). A RIDE button appears only when near a unicorn or mounted. Long-press your portrait for emotes and quick-chat; tap a lane edge to ping. One-thumb alternative in settings: tap-to-move with pathfinding, tap an entity to act on it. Hygiene: touch-action: none on the canvas, double-tap zoom and pull-to-refresh disabled, Screen Wake Lock during a run, AudioContext unlocked synchronously in the Join tap's touchend, fixed-timestep sim so Low Power Mode's 30 fps does not change game speed, DPR capped at 2, reduce-motion and shake-off toggles, hit targets at least 48 px. Desktop: WASD or arrows, Space = DO, E = ride, Shift = charge, 1-4 emotes, Tab = ping; the same square-ish arena is pillarboxed with team stats and the shop in side panels; gamepad via the Gamepad API. Acceptance test: a first-time player on a phone must throw a glizzy to a guest within 10 seconds with no text; if they cannot, the verb is redesigned, not the tutorial.
- **Netcode:** Server-authoritative rooms on Colyseus 0.18 (self-hosted always-on Fly.io machine, roughly $10-20/mo, one region to start) with 20 Hz state patches. Clients send inputs with sequence numbers, predict their own avatar, reconcile on server state, and render everyone else 100 ms in the past interpolating between the last two patches. The simulation (raccoon state machines, wave spawner, grill timers, throws, knockback, Vibe meter, Tips) is one TypeScript module shared by server and client; solo runs it locally. Ragdolls, squash and stretch, particles and unicorn wobble are client-side cosmetics driven by server events, not simulated physics. Supabase handles anonymous auth, profiles and inventory behind RLS, and Realtime only for Crew presence and invites. Vercel serves the static client (first interactive payload under 5 MB, extra arenas and cosmetics lazy-loaded). Reconnect: allowReconnection 60 s, reconnection token in sessionStorage, and on visibilitychange to visible send a ping and reconnect with a full state sync if no pong within 2 s. Latency tolerance: Designed for 150 ms RTT, playable to 250 ms. Raccoon grabs have a 300 ms reach animation before the steal resolves, throws are magnetized to targets, bonk hitboxes are generous and resolved server-side with 100 ms of rewind, and no wave asks for a frame-perfect dodge. The one twitchy verb, the unicorn charge, is predicted locally and snapped only on large divergence.
- **Humor engine:** Comedy comes from three interacting systems, not from written jokes. (1) The raccoon reaction table: every raccoon runs a small state machine (sneak, grab, flee, bonked, taunt, distracted, panicked) and a weighted reaction fires on each transition, always visible to all players and 1-2 s long. Bonked: ragdoll, drop loot, 60% dramatic faint or 40% flee crying. Steals a hat: wears it and performs that player's own emote at them while fleeing. Hit by a thrown glizzy instead of a bottle: catches it, eats it, waves thanks and leaves visibly fatter, so throwing food at raccoons is a failure you laugh at and the game never has to explain the rule. Sees a charging unicorn: freezes and covers its eyes. Picks up a burnt glizzy: spits it and gets angry at the nearest player. Pranks landed while the raccoon is not looking (bonk from behind, snatch the loot back from a taunting one) pay double Tips, the Goose Game rule. (2) Physics that punish sloppiness in funny ways: unicorns have momentum and drift and spook at smoke, riders ragdoll on fence hits, charges knock teammates over (friendly fire is knockback only), saddlebags spill on collision, relish slicks make players slip as well as raccoons, and the two-person Big Glizzy carry wobbles like a R.E.P.O. haul. (3) Over-reacting guests with feedback loops: a crying kid attracts raccoons, a burning grill's smoke pulls every raccoon toward it, Grandpa falls asleep and raccoons take his cosmetic dentures, the reviewer's floating star rating drops when a serve hits her in the face. Because these loops chain, the group authors jokes nobody scripted: a forgotten grill summons a raccoon who eats the burnt dog, gets sick, runs in circles, knocks a rider off a loaded unicorn, and guests and raccoons dive for the spilled glizzies together. Attribution makes it social: every mishap is credited by name on the wave card and there is a blame emote. Dialogue is limited to 2-4 word bubbles, and the 'glizzy' wordplay is never the joke, so the title survives the slang cooling. Playtests count out-loud laughs per wave as the primary metric, target one per player per wave.
- **Signature moments:**
  - The Hat Heist: a Yoinker grabs your hat, puts it on, does your own emote at you and bolts for the treeline; the whole crew abandons the grill to chase it; you bonk it from a unicorn one tile from the fence and the hat pops back onto your head with a jingle while the unattended grill catches fire behind you.
  - Trench Coat Guest: the course-one mini-boss is three raccoons in a trench coat sitting at a picnic table with a 'Plain dog pls' bubble. Serve it and it eats the dog and unzips into three raccoons that scatter with the table's food; throw a mustard bottle at it instead and it topples like a Jenga tower. Crews argue about which one it is every time a new guest sits down.
  - Double Charge: two riders charge from opposite ends of the lawn, collide in the middle, both ragdoll, eight saddlebag glizzies spray across the grass, and guests and raccoons dive for them at once while the third player's bubble just says WHY.
  - Big Glizzy Tug: on the final wave the Bandit King's kits get under the Big Glizzy and walk off with it like a centipede; two players grab the tail end for a wobbling tug of war, one bonks kits off one by one, and the fourth has to keep serving so the Vibe meter does not empty during the standoff.
  - Ketchup Extinguisher: a neglected grill goes up, smoke vacuums every raccoon on the map toward it, and the only extinguisher is a ketchup bottle; putting it out coats everyone within two tiles red for the rest of the wave and the wave card reads 'Who left the grill? [Name]'.
- **Brief fit.** Raccoons: Natural: they are the entire enemy roster and the comedy engine. Eight types from one rig plus props: Trash Panda (grab and run), Yoinker (steals hats off players and wears them), Chonker (three bonks, sits on and squashes placeables), Lid Knight (trash-can-lid shield, must be flanked, so two players), Kit Swarm (five tiny ones carry off a whole picnic table), Skater (fast lane), Parachutist (trash-bag chute, drops anywhere and breaks lane logic), Rustler (steals unattended unicorns). Mini-bosses: Trench Coat (three raccoons posing as a guest), The Lawnmower (ride-on mower loop that mows placeables, stopped by throwing a glizzy into the intake), The Balloon. Boss: the Bandit King on a stolen unicorn who coordinates the Big Glizzy heist. Escalation is absurd, never scary.
  - Unicorns: Mechanically earned, fictionally hand-waved, and I am saying so plainly. The mount is the speed, hauling and crowd-control layer (2.2x speed, 4-slot saddlebags, line-charge knockback, a sparkle trail that slows raccoons for 1 s) and a deliberately scarce shared resource: one post per two players, so 'who rides?' is a negotiation the crew has out loud. Unruly physics (momentum, drift, spooks at smoke, rider ragdolls on fence hits, saddlebag spills on collision) make riding funny with no writing. The fiction is one sentence ('the club's lawn-ornament unicorns wake up when the grill lights') and Jay can replace it. If Jay wanted unicorns to be the star, this angle under-uses them; here they are the second-best toy after throwing, and the novel verb the genre needs (unicorn-mounted glizzy delivery with spills) lives in them.
  - Player creation: A 60-second creator before the first match, available to guests: 3 body shapes, 8 faces, 8 palettes, one free starter hat (so the hat collection starts at 1, not 0), a catchphrase for wave cards, and a pitch for your 'huh' grunt. Outfits render in the lobby and in-round to teammates. The hat is a gameplay object (Yoinkers steal it and wear it), so expression is wired into the systems instead of living in a menu.
  - Collections and outfits: Deterministic and transparent: the Merch Tent lists fixed Bun prices; the Raccoon Field Guide shows 'bonk 10 Chonkers' style unlock text per sticker; the Recipe Book fills as toppings are tried. Everything starts partly filled and nothing is randomized or sold at random. Eight cosmetic slots (hat, face, top, back, unicorn skin, saddle, emote, throw trail) unlock over sessions so identification compounds. If Jay monetizes later it is priced cosmetics with a confirm step and no gameplay effect.
  - Optional profile: First tap creates an anonymous Supabase user; avatar, Buns, cosmetics and stickers are saved server-side under that id and cached in localStorage (wrapped in try/catch). The 'Save your hat?' prompt appears once, after the first cleared course, never before play and never as a gate. Creating a username + password links credentials to the same user id (synthetic email, confirmation off), so nothing is migrated or lost. Guest keeps: all progress on that browser, indefinitely unless site data is cleared (Safari can purge script storage after 7 days without a visit). Guest loses: cross-device sync, recovery after clearing data, a unique reserved name, the Crew roster, and gifting cosmetics (RLS restricts that to non-anonymous users). Password recovery needs an optionally linked real email; without it a lost password is a lost account and the UI says exactly that.
- **Vertical slice:** Players: 1-4 via room code on phone and desktop, guest only (anonymous Supabase user plus localStorage), no account UI. Mode: Quick Snack on one arena (Backyard, 8 lanes), one course of 4 waves plus the Trench Coat mini-boss, about 5 minutes. Enemies: Trash Panda, Yoinker, Trench Coat. Stations: cooler, grill with burn and smoke, bun table, 4 guests of one archetype in 3 palettes. Two placeables in a fixed loadout (mustard turret, relish slick), no shop. One unicorn post with the full mount, saddlebag, charge and spook behavior, because the mount is the novelty being tested. Creator: 3 bodies x 4 faces x 5 hats x 6 palettes, one free hat. Wave-end card with contribution icons and one blooper stat. Four quick-chats and lane pings. Solo via Uncle Ray. Assets: about 30 backyard tiles and 10 props, 1 player paper-doll rig with 8 tween-animated states, 1 raccoon rig with 2 skins and 7 states, 1 unicorn rig with 4 states, 1 guest rig with 3 states, about 8 item sprites, about 25 UI pieces, 15 SFX, 1 music loop; roughly 150 sprites and 16 audio files at placeholder quality. Cut: courses 2-3, all other raccoon types and bosses, the shop, Buns and the Merch Tent, sticker book, accounts, emote dances, second arena, share card, daily goals, settings beyond joystick size and mute. Proof of fun: a group of four strangers to the build laugh out loud at least once per wave and ask for one more without prompting; solo first-course completion above 80%; time-to-first-throw under 30 s on a real iPhone and a mid-range Android over cellular. One developer with AI tooling: 2-3 weeks (scope S for the slice).
- **Content cost:** Slice: about 150 sprites, 16 audio files, 1 tileset, data for 5 waves. Launchable v1: 3 arenas (Backyard, Park, Tailgate Lot; each about 40 tiles and 15 props), 8 raccoon types built from one rig plus props and palettes, 3 mini-bosses and 1 boss with their own rigs, 6 guest archetypes on one rig, 10 placeables, 3 player bodies (deliberately not 5, because every top and back must be drawn per body), 8 faces, 62 cosmetic pieces (14 hats, 8 face items, 10 tops, 6 backs, 6 unicorn skins, 4 saddles, 8 emotes, 6 throw trails) plus body variants, 16 illustrated Field Guide stickers, 36 hand-tuned wave curves plus an endless generator, about 70 SFX and 4 music loops (raccoon chitters and unicorn noises via ElevenLabs SFX generation), about 150 short strings, a share-card template, and lobby, creator, Merch Tent and settings UI. Total roughly 600-800 sprite assets, tween-animated rather than frame-by-frame, and about 75 audio files. Estimate: one 2D artist 8-12 weeks at cartoon quality, or 4-6 weeks with AI-assisted concepting and a strict paper-doll pipeline; audio 1 week; code: engine, netcode and reconnect 6-8 weeks, systems and content 6-8 weeks, juice, mobile QA and accessibility 4 weeks. The slice is roughly 15% of v1 art and 40% of v1 code; the honest v1 total for one developer plus a part-time artist with AI tooling is 4-6 months.
- **Scope:** L (S = weeks, M = 1-3 months, L = 3-6 months, XL = beyond; one developer with AI tooling)
- **Risks:**
  - Fun depends on who is present: a solo or 2-player first session that feels like chores loses the player before they invite anyone; Uncle Ray and headcount scaling are the mitigation, and if solo still tests flat it becomes 'Practice Grill' and the game is marketed as 2-4.
  - Portrait crowding: 4 avatars, up to 12 raccoons, 4 guests and placeables on a 375 px wide screen; entity budget is 30, tiles 48-64 px, hats must read at 24 px; if it does not read, drop to 8 lanes and 3 guests.
  - Cooking becomes busywork: Overcooked-style stations can pull attention from the comedy; cooking is capped at two steps (cook, bun) and toppings stay optional multipliers.
  - Mount contention: one unicorn per two players can cause real friction in some groups; auto-return after 20 s idle and a host toggle for one per player.
  - Netcode on phones: prediction plus knockback on 150 ms cellular networks; server physics stays circles and velocities, ragdolls are cosmetic, and testing happens on cellular, not Wi-Fi.
  - iOS Safari specifics: WebSocket death on backgrounding, undocumented memory ceiling reloads, synchronous audio unlock, no vibrate, no orientation lock, no fullscreen; all known, all need explicit handling and real-device testing rather than devtools emulation.
  - Guest progress evaporating: Safari can purge localStorage and the anonymous session token after 7 days without a visit; the post-course-one 'Save your hat?' prompt is retention-critical, and stale anonymous users need a scheduled cleanup.
  - Genre churn is structural: friendslop titles lose most players within days and see roughly 3% 30-day retention; plan 3-4 named drops, do not budget a live service, and define success as a few evenings per friend group plus steady new groups.
  - Name and IP are unverified: no trademark clearance, no domain or handle check; keep 'Glizzy Lovers' as the full brand, zero gun or eating jokes, and no 'With Friends' naming.
  - Art combinatorics: bodies x tops x backs explodes; enforce 3 bodies, palette tinting, and a paper-doll pipeline from day one.
  - Raccoon scope creep: 8 types is the cap; a new type must add a new player behavior (flank, chase, protect, throw-into) or it is a palette swap.
  - Unknowns from the brief (team size, budget, timeline, age rating, existing lore) can move the scope letter; L assumes one or two developers with AI tooling and a part-time artist.
- **Story needed from the owner:** Jay must provide, about 30 minutes of writing: (1) A tone pick from three: Cozy Cartoon (Animal Crossing energy), Chaotic Slapstick (Goose Game and Overcooked), or Deadpan Absurd (raccoons file HOA complaints), with one line on why. (2) A target age band: Everyone, E10+ or Teen, which sets word filters and whether guests can be mildly rude. (3) A 2-3 sentence premise in Jay's own voice, or a veto of mine: 'Every Saturday the Glizzy Lovers Club hosts the neighborhood cookout. The raccoons of Trashwood Creek have decided it is their cookout now. The club's lawn-ornament unicorns wake up whenever the grill lights.' (4) 3-5 named characters with one trait each, either guests (the reviewer, the sleeping grandpa, the crying kid, the friend who only eats plain) or raccoons (the Bandit King's name and his one line); real-friend cameos are welcome with their permission. (5) 10-20 slapstick things that actually happened at cookouts Jay has been to; these become reaction-table entries and blooper stats. (6) Whether there is an authority figure (the HOA, the Cookout Committee, Uncle Ray) and what they sound like in one sentence, since wave banners and fail screens carry that voice. Not needed for v1: a plot, cutscenes, a lore bible, dialogue trees, character backstories or a story mode; the research (Lazzaro's 'emotion without story', MDA treating Narrative as one of eight aesthetics, and every friendslop hit shipping a premise paragraph plus a quota) says more story does not add fun here. The designer invents and Jay vetoes: the raccoon roster and names, the three arenas, item and topping names, sticker book entries, guest archetypes, quick-chat lines, the Merch Tent catalogue, and all wave banners.

### Pitch 2: Glizzy Lovers: Night Shift

*Get the dogs back. Don't wake the raccoons. Don't feed the unicorn.*

Lethal Company for hot dogs, on your phone, no download. The raccoons of Glizzy County have been robbing every hot dog stand for a month and hoarding the loot in the dump, the storm drains and the county fair after dark. The Glizzy Lovers Mutual Recovery Society (a faceless boss called the Grillmaster on a crackly CB radio) sends 1-4 friends in on foot to sneak the glizzies back and get out on the only transport left: skittish, glowing, glizzy-hungry unicorns borrowed from the fair's petting zoo. Every night has a quota. Every shift has a timer. Your walking speed is your noise level, a raccoon that catches you will steal your hat and wear it, the big prizes need two or four people to carry on a wobbly spring, and the unicorn will eat your cargo if nobody stays with it. The game is a 20-minute, three-shift run joined by a four-letter room code; failure is instant, cheap and always somebody's fault.

- **Core loop.** Seconds: Push the thumb a little to tiptoe (silent), more to walk, all the way to sprint (loud; every raccoon within earshot flips from Snoozing to Curious). Tap the context button to Grab a glizzy off a pile, Hold it to slow-steal one out of a sleeping raccoon's paws (1.5 s ring, breaks if the noise meter spikes), tap again to Bonk a raccoon that is adjacent, drag-and-release to Throw or Pass with target magnetism. Each grab pops a counter, a squelch and a squash-and-stretch on the glizzy; each bonk has 80 ms hit-stop, particles and a yelp; the raccoon reacts visibly (faint, tantrum, flee-with-your-hat) so every 5-second slice has a legible competence hit.
- Minutes: One Shift = 4-6 minutes on one map (Dump, Drains or Fair). Unicorns are tethered at the drop-off. The crew sneaks in, fills pockets (3 glizzies each) and the unicorn saddlebags (10 each), and decides whether to go deeper for the Foot-Long (2-person carry, worth 5) or the Party Platter (4-person carry, worth 15). Two pressures rise together: the Shift Timer (dawn / the trash truck) and the Raccoon Alarm meter (fed by noise, unicorn horn-glow and time). At 100% Alarm the whole map wakes (the Swarm) and the shift becomes a getaway. Extraction = mount up, ride to the Cooler and dismount; anything on a unicorn that crosses the line counts. A ragdolled friend can be dragged out or woken with a thrown glizzy; nothing carried is lost on a knockdown, it just scatters.
- Session: One Night = 3 shifts plus two 60-second Shop breaks (~18-22 minutes total, fits one sitting on a phone). The Grillmaster sets a quota (Night 1: 20 glizzies for a 4-crew, scaled by headcount); between shifts the crew spends the night's haul on one-night gadgets (mustard bottle, horn sock, shiny decoy, walkie, flashlight, second saddlebag). Make quota: 'You keep the job', quota rises ~30%, one-more-night prompt. Miss it: a firing letter ('Please return the unicorn'), a shared Blooper Card (Loudest Lover, Most Hat-Losses, Unicorn Enabler) and an instant Retry with the same crew. No lives, no lockouts, no loss of anything persistent.
- Meta: Relish (deterministic: 1 per glizzy delivered, bonus per quota met) buys outfits, hats, unicorn tack and emotes at fixed, visible prices. The Sticker Book (24 slots, starts at 3/24) fills with raccoon types met, glizzy varieties recovered (Chicago, Chili, Corn Dog, Sonoran, Coney...) and unicorn moods witnessed. Titles come from Blooper Cards and clutch extractions. Crew identity comes free from the room code (Crew BRAT = 'The Bratwurst Bandits'), and a Wanted Board in the lobby shows which raccoons are currently wearing your crew's stolen hats. Guests keep all of this on-device; an account makes it follow them.
- **Players / length:** 1-4 per crew. Tuned for 4 (Party Platter, Chonk push, unicorn-sitter role all exist for 4); complete at 2 (Foot-Long carry, one unicorn, one sitter); solo is a real 'Practice Shift' where the unicorn is your teammate (it follows, you load it, you send it home with cargo). Raccoon density and quota scale with headcount; no bots in v1. / Shift 4-6 min. Night = 3 shifts + 2 shop breaks = 18-22 min with a hard end and a 'one more night' continuation. Time from URL open to controlling your character: under 30 s on a 4G phone (client budget 5 MB initial).
- **Room flow:** Host taps Start a Crew: 4-letter code from an unambiguous alphabet plus a share link (glizzylovers.gg/BRAT). Joiners enter code + name, no account, in the lobby within 10 s; crew name and colours derive from the code. Host picks map and Spice (implicit difficulty: deeper zones are optional in-map anyway). Drop-in during shop breaks; on disconnect the seat is held 90 s and the phone reconnects on visibilitychange with a state sync. Works across desktop and phone in one room.
- **Solo play:** Supported and earns Relish and stickers. Quota is scaled to one, the map's 4-person Platter is absent, the unicorn auto-follows and can be sent to the Cooler with a tap, and the Blooper Card still fires (it roasts you). Solo exists so the first session is never empty, but the lobby's default call to action is 'Invite your crew'.
- **Touch controls:** Portrait, phone-first. Reasons: iOS cannot lock landscape so a landscape design would ship with a nag overlay; the three maps are vertical (drains go down, dump piles go up, the fair is a vertical midway) so a tall viewport shows more of the route; and a portrait phone is one-handed on a couch, which is where friends actually play a room-code game. Visibility is a radius-based flashlight cone, so a desktop player in a wide window does not see farther. Controls: a floating joystick appears wherever the movement thumb lands (either side; the other side gets the action cluster; left/right-handed remembered). Joystick deflection is speed and speed is noise: under 40% tiptoe, 40-80% walk, over 80% sprint, with a noise ring around the avatar so the meaning is visible without reading. One big context button in the opposite bottom corner that relabels itself (Grab / Steal (hold) / Bonk / Mount / Dismount / Push); drag outward from it and release to Throw or Pass with target magnetism; a small Whistle button above it; tap any screen edge to Ping; swipe up on your avatar for the 4-emote wheel. All hit targets 56 px or larger, touch-action: none on the canvas, wake lock during a shift, navigator.vibrate on Android for bonks, reduce-motion and shake-off toggles. A tap-to-move mode is a settings toggle for true one-thumb play. Desktop: WASD (Shift tiptoe, hold Space to sprint-toggle), Space action, E whistle, Q ping, 1-4 emotes, right-click drag to throw; landscape layout on desktop is the same scene with the HUD moved to the corners.
- **Netcode:** Server-authoritative rooms: Phaser 4 client, Colyseus 0.18 room server on an always-on Fly.io machine (or Colyseus Cloud, ~$15/mo) with a 20 Hz tick and 20 Hz state patches; the server owns raccoon AI, the Alarm meter, the shared-carry spring, unicorn appetite and every Relish grant. Client runs a 60 Hz local sim with input prediction and reconciliation for your own avatar and a 100 ms interpolation buffer for everyone else; inputs, not positions, go up the wire. Room code = Colyseus custom-room-id recipe (4 letters, private, registered in presence), reconnection tokens in sessionStorage with a 90 s seat hold. Supabase handles anonymous auth, username/password linking, RLS-protected inventory and Realtime only for lobby presence and invites (never movement). Static client on Vercel; rooms are not on Vercel because its WebSocket functions die at the duration limit. Latency tolerance: Crisp at or under 100 ms RTT; designed to stay fun to about 200 ms and merely 'heavy' rather than broken at 250 ms. Raccoon lunges have a 700 ms wind-up, grabs use a magnetism radius so they never need pixel precision, bonks get 150 ms server-side rewind forgiveness, and the shared carry is a soft spring so lag reads as weight. Rooms are created in the region nearest the host, so a split-continent crew pays the distance but the design never asks for frame-perfect input.
- **Humor engine:** The jokes are outputs of five systems, not lines of text. (1) Noise-is-speed: every player's speed is broadcast as a visible noise ring, so the friend who panics and sprints is instantly, publicly the reason the dump woke up; the Shush emote exists purely so people can point at them. (2) Raccoon reaction matrix: six raccoon types share five over-the-top states (Snooze with snore bubble, Curious head-tilt, Alert chase, Grab-and-wear, Dramatic Faint) triggered by noise, glow, proximity, shiny objects and hits; because triggers stack (a Mama's screech wakes the litter, which alerts a Bandit, which steals the hat of the person who bonked the Mama) the same rules produce fresh disasters. Pranks pay more when the victim is not looking: stealing from a sleeping raccoon and leaving a stick in its paws makes it wake up, inspect the stick, and throw a tantrum that distracts its friends (Goose Game's lesson, Hitman's systems). Bonk one raccoon and two others hold a tiny funeral procession to carry it off, which unguards the nest. (3) Unicorn appetite and physics: floppy neck, bouncy saddle, horn glow, bolting on screeches, and a 20-second unattended timer after which it eats the saddlebag and rainbow-sneezes; the unicorn generates 'who was watching Gus' arguments without a single scripted line. (4) Bodies: players ragdoll on hits and can be dragged; the Foot-Long and Party Platter sit on a soft spring between carriers, so one grabbed teammate swings the whole crew and going through a gap sideways is a group skill nobody has. (5) Failure as punchline: the stolen hat stays on that raccoon for the rest of the night and appears on the lobby Wanted Board; the end-of-shift Blooper Card assigns titles from the actual telemetry (Loudest Lover with a noise graph, Unicorn Enabler, Fell In The Drain x3). The Grillmaster's radio has about 80 short barks, but they are reactive (fired by events with cooldowns, e.g. a unicorn whinny prompts 'Was that a HORSE?'), never a punchline on a timer. Dialogue stays cartoon-short; playtests count laughs and out-loud accusations per shift as the primary metric.
- **Signature moments:**
  - The Hat Heist: a Bandit snatches the hat you built in the creator during extraction, sprints off wearing it, and for the rest of the night you keep seeing YOUR hat bob past on a raccoon; it shows up on the lobby Wanted Board and getting it back earns the 'Hat Return' sticker and a slow-motion re-hat animation.
  - Gus Ate The Quota: the crew parks the unicorn with 9 glizzies in the saddlebag, everyone runs back for 'just one more', and returns to a visibly rounder unicorn, an empty bag, a rainbow sneeze and the quota missed by one; the Blooper Card names the Unicorn Enabler.
  - The Platter Run: four players carrying the 15-glizzy Party Platter down the storm-drain ramp on a spring while a Chonk rolls after them; one carrier gets grabbed, the platter whips the other three into the wall, and someone has to yell which unicorn to mount at the bottom.
  - The Raccoon Funeral: you mustard one raccoon, it faints in slow motion with X eyes, and two of its friends solemnly carry it away past the nest, leaving the pile unguarded for exactly as long as the procession takes; crews start bonking on purpose to trigger it.
  - Dawn Clutch: the timer at 0:03, the Swarm awake, a rider hanging off the side of the unicorn by one hand holding a Foot-Long that a teammate threw from the fence, crossing the Cooler line as the sun comes up; the shift-end card stamps CLUTCH and everyone gets the shared assist credit.
- **Brief fit.** Raccoons: Not forced; they are the whole antagonist ecosystem and the comedy engine. Six systemic types, each with the same five reaction states (Snooze, Curious, Alert, Grab, Faint) plus one signature: Grunt (basic), Bandit (steals what you hold, including your hat, and wears it), Chonk (rolls, needs two players to push), Mama (screech wakes her litter), Magpie (obsessed with shiny things, can be lured with a decoy), Sleeper (only wakes to sprinting). Two bosses: Big Trashley at the Dump, the Kingpin on the Fair's Ferris wheel. Raccoons stealing food at night is the one premise nobody needs explained.
  - Unicorns: The least obvious fit for a heist, so we made them the novel toy instead of a skin: the unicorn is the getaway car AND a liability. It carries 10 glizzies, jumps fences and outruns any raccoon, but its horn glows (raises Alarm in dark zones unless you fit a horn sock), it bolts when a Mama screeches (taking your cargo the wrong way), it has a floppy neck and a bouncy saddle so riding looks stupid, and if nobody stays with it for ~20 seconds it eats the saddlebag contents and rainbow-sneezes. 'Unicorn-sitter' is a real role that a low-skill friend can own. One line of premise covers why: the Society's van is impounded and the fair's petting-zoo unicorns work for glizzies. If playtests show the mount is dead weight, the fallback is to make the unicorn the only way to cross the map's water/fence gates so it is never optional; not a swap-out.
  - Player creation: Players are cartoon humans in the Society's uniform (the hot-dog mascot 'Franklin' lives on the logo and the radio, which anchors the food meaning of the name). A 60-second creator before the first shift, no account needed: 4 body shapes, 12 faces, 10 hair, skin tone, one starter hat (given, not empty), unicorn coat + mane colour, one catchphrase emote. Outfits are visible in lobby and in-round, and on the ragdoll, and on the raccoon that stole your hat. New slots (gloves, saddle, horn cap) unlock over nights so identification deepens.
  - Collections and outfits: Natural, but kept cosmetic-only outside a run to match the genre's light meta. Relish is earned deterministically and every item shows its exact price and how to earn it; no random drops, no paid randomization. Sticker Book starts 3/24 (endowed progress) and is filled by doing things, not buying. Outfit sets have a Proteus nudge: the 'Brave' set (helmet, cape) is an early cheap reward aimed at timid players; the 'Clown' set licenses chaos. In-run gadgets (bought with the night's haul, lost at night end) give the Lethal Company store loop without persistent power creep.
  - Optional profile: First tap creates an anonymous Supabase user; the lobby never asks for anything but a name (blank = random pun name like 'Chili Chad'). Guests keep avatar, unicorn, Relish, outfits, stickers and titles on that device/browser (anon user id + localStorage cache wrapped in try/catch). Guests lose: cross-device play, recovery if they clear site data or used a private tab, gifting Relish/items to crewmates and a named spot on crew boards. 'Save your hat?' appears once, after the first quota met, and links a username + password to the same user id (synthetic email, confirmation off) so nothing resets; an optional real email can be added later for password reset, and the UI says plainly that without one a lost password is a lost account. No nag screens, no held-back features beyond sync and gifting.
- **Vertical slice:** Goal: prove that sneak-grab-carry-extract at 3-4 players on phone browsers produces laughs and 'one more shift'. Players: 2-4 in one room-code lobby (solo allowed but not tuned). One map: the Dump, a single hand-built 60x40 tilemap with three zones (fence line, mid-piles, the Nest) and one Cooler. One shift type (5 min timer + Alarm meter), no Night structure, no shop; end card shows quota met/missed and a three-line Blooper Card. Raccoons: Grunt and Bandit only, with all five reaction states plus hat-stealing. One unicorn (Gus) with follow, mount, saddlebag, bolt-on-screech and the 20-second eat timer. Glizzies: one small type plus the 2-person Foot-Long on the spring. Gadgets: mustard bottle only. Avatar: 3 bodies x 4 hats, chosen in the lobby, guest name only (anonymous Supabase user, no account UI). Controls: floating joystick with speed-is-noise, context button, drag-throw, whistle, ping, Shush and Blame emotes; desktop keys. Netcode: Colyseus room on one Fly.io machine with reconnection tokens. Rough asset count: ~40 unique sprites (~350 frames on a simple layered rig), 1 tileset of ~64 tiles, ~25 SFX, 1 music loop, ~10 radio barks. Cut from the slice: Drains and Fair, bosses, Chonk/Mama/Magpie/Sleeper, Party Platter, account linking, Relish shop, Sticker Book, share cards, unicorn skins, hide-in-dumpster. Success test: four people who have never seen it laughing out loud inside 5 minutes with one friend explaining, and at least one retellable disaster per shift.
- **Content cost:** Slice: about 40 unique sprites (~350 frames), one tileset, ~25 SFX, one music loop, ~10 barks; roughly 2 weeks of art and audio with AI-assisted generation and cleanup, or one week for a working artist. Launchable v1: three maps (Dump, Drains, Fair) built from ~40 modular room chunks so each map has 3 layout variants without hand-placing everything; 6 raccoon types + 2 bosses sharing one animation rig (~700 frames total if hand-animated, ~250 unique parts on a layered rig); one unicorn rig + 8 recolour skins; player rig with 4 bodies, 12 faces, 10 hair, ~40 hats/outfit pieces at launch (~400 layered parts across 4 facings); 12 glizzy types, 6 gadgets, ~30 props, 24 stickers, ~80 UI icons, share-card templates; ~120 SFX, 5 music loops, ~80 Grillmaster barks + firing letters via ElevenLabs. That is roughly 2,000-2,500 frames if fully hand-drawn or 600-800 unique pieces on a rig; call it 6-8 artist-weeks with AI tooling plus 2 weeks of audio, on top of about 10-14 engineering weeks for netcode, three maps, the creator, the economy and accounts. Add 2-3 weeks of playtest tuning because the Alarm meter, unicorn timer and spring stiffness are the whole feel.
- **Scope:** L (S = weeks, M = 1-3 months, L = 3-6 months, XL = beyond; one developer with AI tooling)
- **Risks:**
  - Shared spring carry under 150-250 ms mobile latency may feel mushy or desync; the slice must test it on two real phones over cellular before any other content is built, with the fallback of a 'both carriers hold Action' abstract carry if physics fails.
  - iOS Safari closes the WebSocket on app switch and reloads the page under memory pressure; reconnection tokens, a 90 s seat hold, 2048 px atlases and a texture budget under ~150 MB are launch requirements, not polish.
  - Portrait stealth needs off-screen awareness: without edge indicators for raccoon Alert states and teammate pings, players die to things they could not see and it reads as unfair rather than funny.
  - A solo or 2-player first session that feels empty loses the player before they invite anyone; the unicorn-as-teammate and scaled quota must be fun on their own, and this is unproven until the slice.
  - In-browser voice on mobile Safari is unreliable; the design assumes Discord/FaceTime and ships pings, emotes and the noise ring as first-class stand-ins, which may under-deliver the proximity-chat comedy the genre runs on.
  - Genre churn is structural (AppMagic: ~3% 30-day retention); plan 3-4 named drops (new raccoon boss, new mount, cosmetic season) rather than a live service, and do not promise longevity to anyone.
  - Room hosting cannot live on Vercel; an always-on Fly.io machine or Colyseus Cloud (~$10-20/mo) is an extra bill and ops surface for a one-person team.
  - The 'glizzy' name carries a firearm sense and a TikTok innuendo; any blaster-style gadget or suggestive eating animation could raise the rating or trip store reviews, so gadgets are bottles, socks and decoys and eating is unicorn-only and cartoonish.
  - Trademark, domain and social handles for 'Glizzy Lovers' are unverified; check USPTO and handles before spending on a wordmark.
  - Six raccoon types with five states each is the largest art line; if art time slips, ship 4 types and 1 boss and keep the reaction matrix, because the states are the comedy, not the roster.
  - Team size, budget, timeline, monetization and age rating are unknown; the scope call below assumes one developer with AI tooling, cosmetics-only monetization (if any), and a Teen-or-below tone.
- **Story needed from the owner:** The genre runs on a premise paragraph, a boss voice and a quota; no plot is needed for v1. Jay must provide, in this order of importance: (1) the tone dial in one sentence (how absurd vs cozy, and whether the game says 'glizzy' out loud constantly or plays the word straight as a proper noun); (2) the target age/tone rating (Everyone-10 vs Teen decides whether raccoons can swear-adjacent and how mean the Blooper Cards get); (3) a yes/no/edit on the designer's three-sentence premise (the Glizzy Lovers Mutual Recovery Society, the raccoon crime wave, the petting-zoo unicorns who work for hot dogs); (4) 10-20 slapstick gags Jay personally finds funny, as bullet points, because that list calibrates the whole reaction matrix better than any design doc; (5) any lore already in Jay's head, even a single character name, so it is not accidentally contradicted. Everything else the designer invents and Jay vetoes: the Grillmaster's voice and ~80 barks, map names and layouts, the six raccoon archetypes and two bosses, glizzy varieties, unicorn name (Gus) and moods, outfit sets, crew-name generator, firing letters, sticker names and titles. Total owner writing effort: one page, ideally in one sitting. If Jay wants an actual storyline (chapters, a villain arc, an ending), that is a post-launch drop and nothing in the research says it improves the first hundred hours.

### Pitch 3: Glizzy Lovers: Cookout Run (unicorn derby concept)

*Outrun the raccoons. Don't drop the dogs.*

Glizzy Lovers is a one-thumb, portrait-mode unicorn derby for 2 to 8 friends who join with a four-letter code and no account. Each rider gallops a wobbly rental unicorn up a scrolling course with a stack of hot dogs balanced on their head, trying to reach the Cookout gate before the Trash Tide, a wall of raccoons chasing from the bottom of the screen, eats everything. It looks like a kart racer but is bent into co-op: the crew shares one delivery quota set by a faceless Grillmaster over a crackly PA, placing only earns a ribbon, and when you wipe out you do not die, you land on a friend's saddle as a passenger who leans, grabs dropped dogs and throws items while they steer (Double Dash on a spring-loaded unicorn). Comedy comes from the physics of the stack, the ragdoll passenger, and raccoons that steal your hat and wear it, not from written jokes. A race is 3 to 5 minutes, a full Cookout is three races in about 15 minutes, and losing means the raccoons ate the potato salad, a blooper reel, and a retry with the same code.

- **Core loop.** Seconds: Your unicorn auto-gallops up a lane-less scrolling track. Every 2 to 5 seconds you make one input: drag anywhere to weave around a roadside Lurker raccoon, tap to hop a ketchup slick or a Thief's outstretched paws, swipe up to lob a glizzy at a raccoon (auto-aim magnetism, no precision needed), swipe down to drop a bun bridge or ketchup slick for the crew behind you, or steer through a mustard geyser so the unicorn sneeze-boosts. Every input gets a sound, squash-and-stretch, particles, and a wobble of the glizzy stack on your head that tells you how close you are to dropping one. Ride directly behind a teammate and both of you slipstream (visible speed lines, shared boost sound). Fall behind the Trash Tide line and you get swarmed: ragdoll, hat stolen, and half a second later you land as pillion on the nearest teammate's unicorn.
- Minutes: A race is 3 legs of 60 to 90 seconds separated by Condiment Stations where the Trash Tide pauses 5 seconds so stragglers catch up (implicit rubber-banding). Crew goal: deliver N glizzies to the Cookout gate before the Tide arrives; N scales with headcount. Per-leg beats: a Thief steals someone's hat and taunts from the roadside, a Dumpster King sits on the road and needs two riders to bonk both sides within one second, a spicy side lane offers 3x glizzy pickups guarded by 3x raccoons (players choose their own difficulty). Passengers lean (drag) to nudge the driver's balance, tap to grab dropped glizzies you ride over, and swipe to throw; they re-mount at the next Uni-Stand or when a teammate throws them a Spare Unicorn item. Race ends at the Grill: delivered count vs quota, then a 10-second slow-mo replay of the biggest wipeout on everyone's screen and four ribbons (First to the Grill, Most Dogs Saved, Best Passenger, Silliest Faceplant).
- Session: A Cookout is three races with rising quota, with 30-second Picnics between them where the crew votes the next course, fires emotes, and spends tokens on a hat while the outfit change shows on everyone's screen. Total 12 to 15 minutes. Miss the quota and the raccoons 'slightly ruin' the Cookout (potato salad gone, Grillmaster wails over the PA), a blooper reel plays, and 'Retry Cookout' keeps the room and the code. No lives, no energy, nothing lost. A Cookout Report share card (crew name, dogs delivered, funniest wipeout still frame) closes the session with a 'One more Cookout' button that keeps the same room.
- Meta: Cookout Tokens are earned at a fixed rate per race and spent on exactly the item you pick at a listed price: hats, rider tops, unicorn tack (saddle blankets, horn tips, mane dyes), emotes, sticker slots. The sticker book starts at 2/12 filled ('Joined the Club', 'First Ride'), the first hat is handed out at the end of the first race, and new creator slots unlock every three Cookouts so identification deepens with time. Crew name and colours derive from the room code. No daily streaks, no login rewards, no random drops. Post-launch cadence: three or four named drops a year (new course + new raccoon type + cosmetic set), each one re-spiking play.
- **Players / length:** 2 to 8 in a room; designed to work at 2 and sing at 4. Above 6, the server caps visible unicorns to the nearest 5 plus off-screen arrows so an 8-rider room stays readable on a phone. Quota, Tide speed and raccoon density scale with headcount. / Race 3 to 5 minutes; Cookout (3 races plus two 30-second Picnics) 12 to 15 minutes; join to riding under 30 seconds for a guest; first laugh targeted under 60 seconds.
- **Room flow:** Title screen: Host a Cookout / Join with code / Ride solo. Host gets a 4-letter code (no 0/O/1/I) and a share link; joiners type the code and a name (blank name gets a glizzy pun). Lobby shows every rider's avatar on their unicorn; first-timers get the 60-second creator here. Host taps Start, everyone else taps Ready. Race, Picnic, Race, Picnic, Race, Report card. Drop-in mid-Cookout as a passenger; drop-out or backgrounded phone becomes an auto-pillion for up to 60 seconds and re-syncs on return. 'One more Cookout' keeps the room and code; the crew name persists per code.
- **Solo play:** Solo works as practice and collection play: you plus two Rental Rider bots that are deliberately clumsy and will carry you when you wipe out. Honest: the fun is friend-dependent (Creative Bloq's 'fun comes from the people'), so solo exists to teach the verbs in one minute and let a guest alone still earn tokens, not to be the pitch. The title screen leads with Host and Join.
- **Touch controls:** Portrait, one thumb, because iOS cannot lock orientation, one-handed phone-in-hand is how friends play on a couch or a call, and a vertical scrolling track (3/4 top-down, camera following your unicorn) gives more forward visibility per pixel than landscape. Landscape is supported by widening the visible verge, never required. The whole canvas is the input surface: horizontal drag = steer (relative to where the thumb lands), tap = hop, swipe up = throw, swipe down = drop, tap own avatar bubble = emote wheel. No virtual joystick, no buttons in the play area, all hit targets over 48 px. Settings: steer sensitivity slider, alternative tap-half-of-screen steering, reduce motion, screenshake off, battery saver (30 fps), mute. Client-predicted inputs so response is under 100 ms regardless of network. Engineering notes: touch-action none on the canvas, passive:false touch handlers, 100svh container with safe-area insets, Screen Wake Lock during a race, AudioContext unlocked synchronously on the Join tap, navigator.vibrate on Android for hits, fixed-timestep simulation so Low Power Mode's 30 fps does not change speed. Desktop: arrows or A/D steer, space hop, W or up throw, S or down drop, E emote; mouse drag also steers.
- **Netcode:** Server-authoritative rooms on Colyseus 0.18, one always-on Fly.io machine (~$10 to 20/month) near the first players, second region only if RTT data demands it. Room codes via the Colyseus custom-room-id recipe (presence.sadd, private rooms, joinById). 20 Hz authoritative tick and patches; clients send sequenced inputs (steer, hop, throw, drop, lean, grab), predict their own unicorn at 60 Hz, reconcile on server state, and render other riders and raccoons 100 ms behind with interpolation between the last two patches. Server owns raccoon AI, Tide position, glizzy drops and pickups, quota, ribbons, and token grants (written to Supabase with the service role; RLS lets clients read their own rows only). Supabase handles anonymous auth, account linking, profiles, inventory and lobby presence. Vercel hosts the static client only. allowReconnection(60 s) holds the seat; a dropped rider auto-pillions on the nearest teammate until they reconnect on visibilitychange. Fallback for the slice only: PlayroomKit host-elected rooms, acceptable if that build does not persist tokens. Latency tolerance: Target 100 ms RTT or less, tolerated to roughly 200 ms: auto-gallop plus a lane-less track means a 150 ms error is under one unicorn length; raccoon pounces have a 600 ms wind-up and hitboxes that favour the rider; item hits are resolved on the server with a predicted client flash that reconciles silently; placing only decides ribbons, so no lag compensation for fairness at launch. Jitter is absorbed by a two-patch interpolation buffer; base latency matters more than jitter, so the game buffers rather than chasing the last few milliseconds.
- **Humor engine:** Three systems, no scripted gag fires twice the same way. (1) Wobble: the unicorn has a loose neck and spring saddle, the rider is a ragdoll on a spring, the glizzy stack is a physics chain, and a passenger hangs off the side; any collision propagates through all four, so a small bump can end with a hot dog on a raccoon's head. Movement itself is funny (Octodad's lesson) but the base steer stays precise so it never becomes frustrating. (2) React: raccoons have five over-the-top reaction states (panic, tantrum when a glizzy sails over their head, steal-and-taunt wearing your hat, dramatic faint from a unicorn sneeze, pile-up when they trip over each other) fired by systemic triggers, plus raccoon-on-raccoon theft; the Grillmaster PA (ElevenLabs voice) has a bank of about 80 short reactions triggered by events, never by a timer, and escalates as the quota gets close. (3) Replay: every wipeout gets a one-second local slow-mo on the victim's screen, and the round's biggest gets a ten-second replay on everyone's screen with a ribbon and a blame button, so failure is a shared punchline rather than a setback. Pranks are rewarded when the victim is not looking: bonking a Thief from behind before it turns gets a bonus sticker chime. Dialogue stays cartoon-short; the word 'glizzy' is a proper noun in the world, not the joke.
- **Signature moments:**
  - The hat heist: a Thief raccoon snatches your hat, puts it on, and taunts from the roadside while your name tag reads '(hatless)'. Next leg you spot it in your hat, bonk it from behind, the hat pings back onto your head with a chime and the raccoon cries. Your friends saw the whole thing.
  - The Double Dash faceplant: you wipe out, land pillion on your friend's unicorn, they hit a ramp, both of you launch, the whole glizzy stack rains down across the road, and the slow-mo replay shows both faces mid-scream while a third friend rides through collecting your dogs.
  - The Trash Tide comeback: final leg, the Tide two seconds behind, one friend drops a bun bridge over the gap, you slipstream through the mustard geyser, the unicorn sneezes, and you cross the Grill with one glizzy left, hitting the quota by exactly one.
  - The Dumpster King two-bonk: the boss raccoon sits on a dumpster lid in the road; two riders hit both sides within a second, it faints dramatically, the lid pops and sprays hot dogs that the whole crew scrambles for while the passenger grabs the most.
  - The Glizzy Pile: four friends hit the same ketchup slick, spin in sync, and end up in a squeaking unicorn pile in the ditch. The PA announces 'That's a Glizzy Pile' and the crew earns the Glizzy Pile sticker, which only exists for rooms of four or more.
- **Brief fit.** Raccoons: Not forced; they are the whole antagonist system. The Trash Tide is the chasing wall that sets the race pressure. Roadside Lurkers pounce with a 600 ms telegraph. Thieves steal hats and wear them while taunting from the verge. Bombers hurl trash. The Dumpster King is the two-player boss. Raccoons carry the comedy: each has a small reaction set (panic, tantrum, steal-and-taunt, dramatic faint, pile-up) fired by systemic triggers, and they react to each other (a Thief who stole your hat can have it stolen by another Thief).
  - Unicorns: Not forced; the unicorn is the vehicle and the physical toy. Rental unicorns from the Club's stable with a loose neck, a spring saddle and a ragdoll rider; the glizzy stack on the rider's head is a physics chain, so every bump wobbles all three. Sneeze-boost in mustard, spin-out on ketchup, slipstream behind a friend. Skins, manes, horn tips and tack are the cosmetic layer. The wipeout-to-pillion rule means unicorns are also the revive mechanic.
  - Player creation: 60-second creator available to guests before the first race: body shape (3), colour, face tiles (eyes, mouth), hat, top, catchphrase emote, unicorn colour and mane. Big heads so hats read at phone size. Outfits show in the lobby and in the race; teammates see your hat get stolen. Creator saved to localStorage for guests and to Supabase for accounts. New slots (saddle charm, horn tip, second emote) unlock over time.
  - Collections and outfits: The one element that would be forced if it were power; here it is cosmetic and grounded by the hat mechanic. Hats are the item raccoons steal, so the Hat Rack is the collection players care about. Sticker book (24 at v1, starts 2/12), Hat Rack, Unicorn Tack, Emotes. Everything deterministic: earn tokens per race, each item shows its price and how to get it; no random drops, no near-miss animations, nothing randomized for money.
  - Optional profile: First tap creates a Supabase anonymous user; guest keeps avatar, outfits, stickers, tokens and crew history on that browser (localStorage plus the anonymous row, wrapped in try/catch for private mode). Guest loses: cross-device sync, recovery if site data is cleared, and appearing on the friends list. 'Save your hat?' appears once after the first finished Cookout, never as a nag. Creating an account links username plus password to the same user id, so nothing restarts. Username-only accounts use a synthetic email, so the game states plainly that a lost password is a lost account and offers optional real-email linking for recovery.
- **Vertical slice:** Two to four players, one course (Backyard to Cookout, three legs, about 3 minutes), one Cookout of a single race, guest-only join by four-letter code, no accounts, no shop. Included because they prove the fun: auto-gallop steer/hop/throw/drop, glizzy stack physics, slipstream, wipeout-to-pillion with lean/grab/throw, Trash Tide chase, Lurker and Thief raccoons (hat theft is in), Condiment Station catch-up, shared quota, one slow-mo replay, four ribbons, a 20-second creator with 3 bodies and 3 hats, Android vibrate, reduce-motion toggle. Rough asset count: unicorn back view with lean left/right (about 12 frames) tinted to 4 colours, rider 3 bodies plus 3 hats, 2 raccoon types with pounce/steal/taunt/faint (about 24 frames), Tide wall (1 looping strip), 3 items, about 40 track tiles and props, 15 UI icons; roughly 150 sprite frames total. Audio: 15 SFX, one music loop, 10 Grillmaster lines from ElevenLabs. Stack: Phaser 4.2 client on Vercel, Colyseus 0.18 on one Fly.io machine, Supabase anonymous auth only. Cut: sticker book, token shop, accounts, Dumpster King, solo bots, versus toggle, other courses, Picnics, share card, Discord. Pass criteria in a four-phone room: first laugh under 60 seconds, one clip-worthy moment per race, first-time player performs steer and hop within 10 seconds without reading, and the room asks for one more race. ### Pitch 4: Glizzy Lovers: Cookout Cover-Up

*One of you is a raccoon.*

Glizzy Lovers is a 4-10 player browser party game in which a club of cartoon hot-dog people throws a cookout and one or two of them are raccoons zipped into hot-dog costumes. The Lovers race the sun to get the cookout ready (grill, cooler, buns, unicorn chores) while the raccoons tip trash, stampede the unicorns and stuff Lovers into cans; anyone who finds a discarded bun calls a Grill Court where the group argues and ejects a suspect by unicorn punt. The comedy is systemic: raccoons physically cannot stay away from trash, mustard leaves footprints, unicorns knock hats onto the wrong heads, and every wrong ejection becomes a shared punchline. Join by 4-letter code on a phone or laptop with no account; an optional username/password profile keeps your outfit, unicorn and sticker book.

- **Core loop.** Seconds: Thumb on the lower half of the screen moves your hot dog (floating joystick or tap-to-move). Every 5-15 s you reach a task pad and play a one-thumb mini-game: tap in rhythm to flip dogs, drag ice into the cooler, swipe buns off the shelf, hold to refill the mustard. Each completed task pops a chunk onto the shared Cookout Readiness bar with a sizzle, confetti and a teammate-visible 'nice' sticker (competence plus relatedness beat). Between tasks you make micro-decisions: cut through Trash Alley (fast but suspicious), borrow a unicorn (2x speed, but it leaves a 15 s rainbow trail everyone can see), or shadow someone you doubt. A raccoon spends the same seconds pretending: doing real tasks, watching the Trash Urge meter fill, waiting for one Lover to be alone, holding CAN for 0.8 s, then deciding whether to walk away or 'find' the bun and report it themselves.
- Minutes: A round is 4-7 min and ends by one of: Readiness hits 100% (Lovers win), raccoons equal Lovers (raccoons win), an unanswered sabotage timer expires (Torches Out unrelit for 45 s = raccoons win), or Sundown at 6:00 with Readiness under 100% (raccoons win). Meetings interrupt: a bun report or the Grill Bell (one per player) freezes the map and opens Grill Court: 45 s discussion (text chat plus one-tap claim chips like 'I saw [Kim] at [Trash Alley] just now'), 20 s vote, then the ejection: the unicorn carries the loser to the fence and punts them; 'was a RACCOON' (costume unzips, a raccoon scurries off with a hot dog) or 'was NOT a raccoon. Was just like that.' Canned or ejected players become Ghost Glizzies who keep doing tasks and can boo NPC raccoons off tables, so nobody sits out.
- Session: A session is 3-5 rounds in the same lobby, 25-35 min, roles reshuffled each round with a private per-phone reveal. After every round a 20 s scoreboard hands out silly titles (Most Sus, Trash Enjoyer, Mustard Victim, Right Call, Cold Case) and a share card ('Round 3: JAY was the raccoon. Caught by mustard footprints.'). A 15 s 'One more?' countdown restarts with everyone still present; late friends join at the next round start.
- Meta: Every round pays Relish Tokens (10 for playing, +5 per title, +5 for a team win, nothing lost for losing) spent in the Wardrobe on exactly the item you point at: buns, sausage skins, condiment hairdos, hats, unicorn coats and saddles, ejection animations, meeting emote packs. A 24-slot Raccoon Sighting sticker book starts at 3/24 and fills from in-round events (get canned inside a dumpster, ride a unicorn during a stampede, win as the last Lover standing), each with its earn condition printed on the slot. Guests keep all of this on the device; the optional account carries it across devices. No dailies, no streaks, no timers, no randomized purchases.
- **Players / length:** 4-10 in one room. 4-6 players = 1 raccoon, 7-10 = 2 raccoons. Sings at 6-8. Works at 4 but deduction is thin. At 2-3 the host can fill to 5 with bots (wanderers that do tasks; one can be the raccoon), which is practice, not the real game. / Round 4-7 min (6:00 Sundown cap plus meeting time); session 3-5 rounds, 25-35 min. One round fits the 5-6 min median mobile session with a built-in 'one more'.
- **Room flow:** Host taps Host Cookout -> 4-letter code from an unambiguous alphabet (no 0/O/1/I) plus a share link with ?room=CODE -> friend opens the link in any browser, types a name (blank = random pun like 'Frank N. Beans'), gets the 60 s creator (skippable, randomized default) -> lands in a playable lobby (walk around, poke the unicorns, try one task pad, see everyone's outfit) -> host sets discussion time, chat mode (text / chips only / 'we are on Discord') and kid-safe filter -> Start -> private role reveal on each phone -> round -> scoreboard -> 15 s 'One more?' countdown. Target: tap to controlling your hot dog in under 30 s. A dropped phone keeps its seat for 120 s and rejoins the same round with a full state sync; an absent player auto-skips in votes; if every raccoon is gone for 120 s the round ends as a Lover win.
- **Solo play:** There is no real solo game; social deduction needs people. What exists: a 90 s Raccoon Range tutorial (learn every task, shoo three NPC raccoons; pays the first 3 stickers and a hat) and Practice Cookout with 5 bots to learn the map and the meeting UI. Be honest on the title screen: 'Best with 6-8 friends'.
- **Touch controls:** Portrait-first, one thumb. Reasons: meetings and chat are text-message shaped and nobody rotates a phone to type; one-thumb play works standing on a bus; iOS cannot lock landscape anyway. The map is drawn inside a fixed circular vision radius, so a portrait phone and a wide desktop window see the same amount of world and nobody gets an information edge. Layout: the lower 55% of the screen is a floating joystick zone (the joystick spawns under wherever the thumb lands; size slider in settings) with a tap-to-move toggle as the alternative scheme; one 64 px context Action button bottom-right whose label changes (USE / REPORT / SHOO / MOUNT / RUMMAGE, and CAN in red for raccoons) so there is never more than one button to think about; a small map button and the Grill Bell live on the map screen. Tasks are full-screen one-thumb mini-games (tap timing, drag, swipe, hold), never two-finger. Grill Court is a portrait list of portraits: tap a portrait to accuse or vote, chat drawer slides up, big VOTE / SKIP. Landscape works too (HUD re-flows, joystick zone becomes the left half) but is never required. Engineering: touch-action: none on the canvas, 100svh with safe-area insets, Screen Wake Lock during rounds, AudioContext unlocked on the 'Tap to join' touchend, navigator.vibrate on Android for canning and stampede hits, reduce-motion and shake-off toggles. Desktop: WASD/arrows, E for action, R to report, Tab for map, Enter for chat, 1-9 for claim chips, mouse for mini-games; it should feel like Among Us on PC.
- **Netcode:** Server-authoritative but lightweight: one room process per lobby (Colyseus 0.18 on a single always-on Fly.io machine, ~$10-20/mo, using the custom-room-id recipe for 4-letter codes and allowReconnection for phones; Cloudflare Durable Objects via partyserver is the near-zero-idle-cost alternative if you are willing to hand-roll state sync). Clients send position or input at 10 Hz; the server clamps speed, applies vision-radius interest management (you only receive positions of players inside your vision circle, plus rainbow trails), and validates CAN range and hold time, task completion, sabotage cooldowns, reports, votes and the round timer. Roles live only in server memory and are sent to each phone privately. Meetings are lock-step turn-based. Supabase handles anonymous and linked auth, profiles, wardrobe and sticker rows behind RLS, and lobby/friends presence; Supabase Realtime is not the movement channel. Static client on Vercel. Latency tolerance: Comfortable up to 250 ms RTT, playable to ~400 ms. Remote players are rendered ~150 ms in the past with interpolation; your own hot dog is predicted locally. The only latency-sensitive action is CAN, which uses a 0.8 s hold and a 1.5-tile radius validated on the server, so 100 ms one way never changes the outcome. Tasks are local mini-games that only report completion; votes and chat are reliable messages.
- **Humor engine:** Comedy comes from five systems that fire off player behaviour, not from written lines. (1) Trash Urge: every raccoon player has a hidden meter that fills over ~90 s; at 80% their hot dog starts glancing at trash cans (a subtle idle animation anyone watching closely can notice), at 100% they must rummage a trash can within 10 s or the costume slips: the tail pops out for 0.7 s with a chitter audible inside the vision radius. Rummaging is an action Lovers also do for bonus tokens and lost hats, so hanging around trash is plausible but suspicious; canning someone also resets the meter, which pushes a passive raccoon to act. Result: 'why were you at the trash cans three times' is generated by the rules, and a tail popping out in front of the grill line is a group scream every night. (2) Mustard: squirting a player paints them yellow for 45 s and leaves footprints for 20 s. Lovers use it to tag suspects, raccoons use it to frame; footprints from a rattling dumpster to a fresh bun are player-authored evidence, and a room of six yellow hot dogs arguing is the natural end state. (3) Unicorn physics: rides leave rainbow trails (public routes), Walk the Unicorn drags two players around by an animal with slightly unruly steering, and the Stampede sabotage ragdolls anyone in the path and launches hats that land on other players' heads. Hats are how people identify each other in the dark, so a stampede creates real mistaken-identity accusations; a knocked-down raccoon also has a 20% chance of a tail flash. (4) Over-reacting NPC raccoons: spawned by Tip Trash, they sprint at tables, steal a hot dog or a hat, taunt, and when shooed drop everything and either flee screaming or dramatically faint. The Grillmaster NPC (owner names them) narrates sabotage over a crackly PA with 12 short panicked barks mixed by situation ('WHO LET THE UNICORNS OUT'), kept short so they do not wear out. (5) Failure as punchline: getting canned is a hot dog stuffed into a trash can with legs wiggling and a clank; the wrong ejection ends in a unicorn punt and 'was NOT a raccoon. Was just like that.'; ghosts boo NPC raccoons so the dead still cause chaos; the scoreboard gives the most-accused innocent 'Most Sus' and the player who walked past a bun longest 'Cold Case'. Copy rules from the name research: no guns (the mustard is a squeeze bottle), no 'gobbler', eating animations are side-chomps, and 'Glizzy' is a proper noun (the Glizzy Lovers club), not the joke. Playtest metric: laughs per round and clips per session, not completion rate.

### Pitch 5: Glizzy Lovers: Raccoon Rush

*Four friends, one hot dog stand, a park full of raccoons and a company unicorn nobody asked for. Don't drop the bun.*

Glizzy Lovers is a 2-4 player co-op kitchen-chaos game you open from a link on your phone. You and your friends work a park hot dog stand for a franchise that never shows its face: a walkie-talkie voice called the Regional Manager sets a tip quota for every 4-minute shift and makes you wear "Glizzy Lovers" loyalty-club aprons. Orders scroll in on a ticket rail; you grill, bun, top and serve by tapping stations and flicking ingredients to each other, while raccoons sneak in from the treeline to steal buns, condiments and, if you are not looking, your hat. One of you rides Gary, the stand's franchise-issued unicorn, on a delivery loop to far-off picnic blankets, balancing a wobbling tray of dogs while the others flick refills onto it as it gallops past. Every mistake is physical and public: a mistimed toss puts mustard in a friend's face, a spooked unicorn launches six orders into the lake, a raccoon does a victory lap in your hat. Hit quota, keep the job, spend tips on outfits; miss it and the Manager reads a firing letter built from your stats, someone taps Rehire, and you go again. Overcooked's cooperative pressure rebuilt for one thumb in portrait, with raccoons as the comedy engine instead of a burning kitchen.

- **Core loop.** Seconds: Glance at the ticket rail (top of screen), tap a station tile to walk there, hold to grab, tap the next station to use it, flick toward a teammate to toss, tap a raccoon in reach to bonk it (it ragdolls and drops the loot), tap the window to serve. Rider: drag thumb sideways to keep the tray level, tap a blanket as you pass to deliver, tap a tree-raccoon before it drops onto the tray. Every tap gets a sound, squash-and-stretch and a floating number or emote reply within 100 ms.
- Minutes: A shift is four 60-second rushes with a 10-second breather between. Rush 1: plain dogs, two Sneak raccoons. Rush 2: toppings unlock, a Grabber shows up, Gary's loop opens. Rush 3: spicy orders (bigger tips, more steps), Sir Lids arrives and needs bonking from behind. Rush 4: the Party Glizzy order (a two-person floppy carry) and a mini-boss. Under the rail sit the shared quota bar and the Picnic Mood meter (shared health that drains when customers wait); the crew argues who rides, who grills, who guards the ketchup.

- **Humor engine:** The comedy is produced by three systems colliding, not by written jokes. ### Pitch 6: Glizzy Lovers — wildcard concept "The Grand Delivery": unicorn-drawn co-op hot-dog hauling

*One giant hot dog. Four unicorns. Too many raccoons.*

Glizzy Lovers is a mobile-first browser co-op for 1-4 friends. Each of you rides a rented unicorn that is roped to the same wobbly cart carrying the Grand Glizzy: a parade-float-sized hot dog with a face who is terrified of everything. Your job is to haul him from the grill to the 6 PM cookout before the bun goes soggy, while raccoons lurk in from the edges to steal toppings, sit on the cart, spook the unicorns and wear your hat. Because every rope pulls the same object, movement itself is the co-op: pull together and the cart glides, pull apart and it twangs, spins and tips, and the hot dog screams. One thumb steers, one tap bonks. A delivery is 3-5 minutes, a run is three deliveries (~15-20 min) with a rising quota from a boss you only hear over a walkie-talkie, and failing just earns your crew a two-star cookout review, never a lost save.

Why this angle and not the other four: (1) Physics climbing (PEAK / Chained Together) is the closest cousin, but climbing needs precision input and multi-hour sessions; a heavy shared cart on a route keeps the physics comedy and the shared burden while tolerating one-thumb steering, 3-5 minute rounds and 150 ms phone latency. (2) A Jackbox-style minigame pack fits phones, but it needs a shared screen, costs a whole game per minigame, and its comedy is scripted prompts rather than systems; raccoons and unicorns would be skins, not mechanics. (3) Asymmetric raccoons-vs-glizzies tag makes a friend the villain, leans on competition (the motivation Quantic Foundry shows declining fastest with age, and which Emmerich & Masuch link to aggression), and breaks at 2 players. (4) The async cookout-builder with raids maps neatly onto Supabase but is not friendslop (friends are not in the room together), invites appointment mechanics, and its live raids still need real-time netcode, so it saves nothing technically. This concept keeps the best organ of each: R.E.P.O./Chained Together's shared burden, Jackbox's room-code flow, raccoons as NPC comedians, and a decorate-your-cart calm mode from the builder as a later Serious Fun layer.

- **Core loop.** Seconds: Drag anywhere (a floating stick spawns under your thumb) to steer your unicorn; because your rope is tied to the cart, steering is pulling. Tap to bonk the nearest raccoon with your bun bat (auto-aim, big radius, hit-stop, particles, yelp). Hold half a second and release for a Sparkle Boost dash that yanks the cart; four synchronized boosts launch it up a hill, one solo boost spins it. Ride over spilled toppings to magnet them back on. Every 10-15 seconds something reacts: a rope twangs, a raccoon faints, the Grand Glizzy's face changes, a teammate falls off their saddle.
- Minutes: One Delivery (3-5 min): haul the cart from the grill to the picnic line along a route with two forks (Safe: longer and calmer; Spicy: shorter, more raccoons, bonus Relish). Raccoon pressure scales with distance covered and headcount; a soggy-bun meter driven by rain clouds is the clock. Keep toppings on, keep the cart upright, bonk raccoons before they reach the bun, chase down thieves. Cross the picnic line: confetti, cheer, delivery graded on toppings intact and time to spare.
- Session: A Cookout Run = 3 deliveries, ~15-20 min including 20-second Topping Stops between them where the crew votes on the next fork, sees 'what you did for the crew' (pulls, bonks, toppings saved, revives, hats recovered) and one auto-captured blooper with a self-deprecating blame vote. The Grillmaster's quota rises each delivery (more toppings must survive). Delivery 3 adds the Bin Baron, a boss raccoon that needs two players to shoo. Failing the quota ends the run with a 1-5 star cookout review and a shareable firing letter; passing unlocks Overtime (one more, harder) with a 'one more?' prompt. Late friends join at any Topping Stop. Nothing collected is ever lost.
- Meta: Relish earned per delivery (price shown on every item, no randomness) buys hats, tops, unicorn coats and manes, horn sounds, emotes and cart decorations. A Topping Sticker Book (starts 2/12 filled) completes by delivering specific toppings intact; a Raccoon Field Guide fills as you bonk each archetype. Crew name and colours derive from the room code so a regular group gets an identity for free. An optional username + password syncs all of it across devices. Roadmap is 3-4 named content drops (new route, new raccoon, cosmetic season), not a daily live service.
- **Players / length:** 1-4 in v1 (2 recommended minimum, 4 is the design target; solo uses a bot unicorn). Cart mass, raccoon spawn rate, Chonk frequency and quota all scale with headcount so a 2-player run is complete, not degraded. 5-6 only in a later drop if rope physics stays legible in portrait. / Delivery 3-5 min; Cookout Run 3 deliveries ~15-20 min with a hard end; optional Overtime. From tap to controlling your unicorn under 30 s on 4G.
- **Room flow:** Host taps 'Host a Cookout' and gets a 4-letter code (no 0/O/1/I) plus a share link. Joiners open the link or type code + name (blank name gets a random pun like 'Relish Randy'); no account, no reading. The lobby is a toy: a parking lot where you can already ride, bonk hats off friends and honk. Host taps Start; the first delivery has slowed raccoons and on-object hints (rope glows, arrow to picnic). Topping Stops between deliveries handle route vote, crew scoreboard and blooper. Drop-in/drop-out: seat held 60 s on disconnect (Colyseus allowReconnection), rejoiners re-hitch automatically; late friends join at any Topping Stop. One code works across phone and laptop.
- **Solo play:** Fully playable: one NPC rental unicorn ('Steve') is auto-hitched and pulls straight ahead, raccoons scale down, and the sticker book still fills, so a solo first session is a real tutorial rather than an empty lobby. It is deliberately the demo, not the point; the share link sits on the results screen with 'this is funnier with three more people'.
- **Touch controls:** Portrait, one thumb, camera locked on the cart so everyone sees the same thing and callouts work. Drag anywhere: a floating stick spawns where the thumb lands and steers the unicorn relative to the cart. Tap = bonk (auto-aim). Hold-release = boost. Double-tap = honk. Swipe down/up on the rope pill = unhitch/hitch. Long-press = emote wheel. No fixed joystick, no multi-finger requirement; an optional two-thumb mode puts bonk/boost buttons bottom-right. Portrait because iPhone Safari cannot lock landscape, one-handed play works standing at an actual cookout, and the route runs up the screen so progress is legible. Canvas sized with 100svh plus safe-area insets, touch-action: none, Screen Wake Lock during a run, AudioContext unlocked synchronously on the Join tap, navigator.vibrate on Android for hits, reduce-motion and shake-off toggles, fixed-timestep simulation so 30 fps Low Power Mode does not change game speed. Desktop: same cart camera with a wider 16:9 view (the laptop player naturally becomes the spotter), WASD/arrows steer, Space bonk, Shift boost, E hitch, Q honk, Tab emotes; mouse click also bonks.
- **Netcode:** Server-authoritative rooms on Colyseus 0.18 (self-hosted always-on Fly.io machine, one region at launch), 20 Hz tick and patches. The server owns the cart body, ropes, raccoon AI, topping state and scoring; clients send inputs with sequence numbers, predict their own unicorn, reconcile on patches, and render remote unicorns, raccoons and the cart ~100 ms in the past interpolating between snapshots. Room codes via Colyseus's custom-room-id recipe with private rooms; reconnection tokens in sessionStorage; on visibilitychange to visible, ping and reconnect within the seat-hold window. Supabase handles anonymous auth, username/password linking, RLS-protected profiles/inventory/stickers (auth.uid() wrapped in select, user_id indexed) and Realtime Presence only for the crew/friends list, never movement. Rewards are written by the game server with a service-role key, never by client inserts. Static client (Phaser 4) on Vercel with brotli and a sub-5 MB first screen. Latency tolerance: Target <=100 ms RTT, playable to ~200 ms. The shared object is deliberately heavy and damped so it responds to force integrated over ~300 ms; a late input reads as 'the cart is heavy', not lag. Bonks have a large radius and hit-stop that hides 100 ms; raccoon snatches have a 600 ms wind-up so no frame-perfect reaction is ever required. Above ~250 ms the rope twang starts to feel unfair, so show a connection pip and prefer a stable interpolation buffer over chasing milliseconds. Jitter matters less than base latency.
- **Humor engine:** Four systemic sources; no scripted jokes fire inside a delivery. (1) Rope physics: every rope is a spring, the cart is a lumpy body with a high centre of mass; opposing pulls twang, spin and tip it, and a tipped cart sheds toppings in an arc that raccoons sprint for. (2) Raccoon reaction states driven by triggers, not scripts: lurk only while no rider faces them (Untitled Goose Game's 'funnier when the victim isn't looking', inverted so turning your back is the setup), snatch, taunt (wave the item, wiggle), flee, faint stiff as a board with tongue out when bonked, tantrum when they drop the item, dogpile when three are near, wear stolen hats and refuse to give them back. (3) Unicorn unruliness (Octodad's lesson): floppy neck, loose saddle, a buck on every Spooker scream, solo boosts that spin the cart, a jump that flings the rider; chain reactions (scream -> buck -> rope yank -> tip -> raccoons pile on) are player-authored and never repeat exactly. (4) The cargo is an over-reacting NPC: the Grand Glizzy has six face states driven by threat, tilt and speed, and squeaks; players will tip the cart on purpose just to hear him. Failure is the punchline: a 1-5 star cookout review, a firing letter, a photo of the raccoon wearing your hat, a 'silliest fall' stat, and an auto-captured blooper with a blame vote framed as affectionate. The only written comedy is ~40 short walkie-talkie lines from the Grillmaster (ElevenLabs voice) triggered by game state and rotated, plus pun names and review copy. Playtests count out-loud laughs per delivery; target three or more.
- **Signature moments:**
  - The Launch: the crew holds four boosts at the top of Picnic Hill and lets go together; the cart clears the duck pond, the Grand Glizzy screams the whole arc, lands upside down on the far bank, and three raccoons immediately sit on it. The auto-clip fires because tilt and airtime both peak.
  - The Hat Heist: a Hat Thief lifts your custom cowboy hat and wears it; you unhitch to chase it into the bushes; down one puller, the cart rolls backwards into Dumpster Alley while your friends yell your name; you come back with the hat and no toppings.
  - Chonk Rodeo: a Chonk plants itself on the bun and four boosting unicorns cannot move the cart; someone dismounts, walks up and honks; the Chonk flees, the stored rope tension snaps the cart forward and it bowls over the Grabber sneaking in from the other side ('Strike!' stat, three raccoons fainting in a row).
  - The Faint: you bonk a raccoon mid-taunt; it goes stiff with its tongue out, slides down the hill on its back and is carried off by two other raccoons like a fallen comrade, while a fourth quietly takes the mustard behind you.
  - Delivery by Inches: rain has the bun at 96% soggy, the cart is on two wheels, one friend is unhitched wrestling a raccoon for the last relish, the other three pull and cross the picnic line with 0.4 s left; the Grillmaster says 'acceptable' and the review card reads 'Toppings: some. Vibes: immaculate.'
- **Brief fit.** Raccoons: The only enemy faction and the whole comedy engine. Five archetypes, each with one behaviour and one over-reaction: Grabber (lurks until nobody faces it, snatches a topping, taunts by waving it), Chonk (climbs on the cart and sits; adds one puller's worth of weight), Spooker (screams; every unicorn in earshot bucks and yanks its rope), Hat Thief (steals your cosmetic hat and wears it until bonked), Bin Baron (boss; hurls bin lids that take two players to shove aside). All faint stiffly when bonked and throw a tantrum when they lose an item. Absurd, never scary. Not forced: the game does not exist without them.
  - Unicorns: Your body and the cart's engine. Friendslop drop-in should be immediate and the arrival itself should be a gag; the lurch also tells the crew without text that someone joined.

What none of the pitches got right, per this judge: What none of the six got right on this lens:

1. Co-op headcount. No cooperative pitch supports 6; the only two that go past 4 are a race and a social-deduction game, both non-friendslop. R.E.P.O. ships 1-6 and a friend group on a Discord call is frequently five or six; "two of you watch" kills the night. The winner must answer 6 in v1 (two carts, a second rope per side, spotter roles), not defer it.

2. The silent group is treated as the fallback, but on mobile browser it is the majority case (a friend opens a link on a bus, iOS Safari voice is unreliable, nobody is on Discord). Every pitch ships pings and emotes as a stand-in; none designs a mechanic that is funnier without voice, and none makes the no-voice room the primary slice pass criterion. Proximity chat is the genre's comedy engine and nobody proposed a real replacement for it, only a substitute.

3. Leaving mid-session is answered with a seat hold, not a gameplay behaviour, everywhere except Cookout Run's auto-pillion. Physically coupled designs (cart, chain, two-person carries) need the frozen body to become a joke (the unicorn wanders off and eats, the rider becomes a passenger) rather than a stall.

4. No spectator or audience mode. Jackbox's audience idea is in the digest and nobody used it: the fifth to eighth friend who does not fit should still be in the room from a phone, watching, voting on the blooper, or dropping one raccoon per delivery. Friendslop clip culture is spectator culture.

5. The meta is individual and identical across all six (Relish/Buns/Tokens, a sticker book starting at 2/12 or 3/24, one 'Save your hat?' prompt). The pitches converged on the research template and nobody proposed crew-level persistence: a crew hat rack of recovered hats, crew stats across nights, a named nemesis raccoon that keeps your hat until the crew gets it back next Friday. The friendslop return lever is the group scheduling the next night, not the individual's closet.

6. All six have the hat-stealing raccoon (convergence is a signal it is right), but none lets that specific raccoon persist across sessions as a nemesis. "We finally got Jerry" is a retell engine nobody built.

7. Clip capture is all automatic (physics peaks, quota drains, share cards). A one-tap "THAT" button any player can press to mark the last ten seconds is missing; it is the cheapest clip tool, it is a social act (everyone sees who pressed it), and it doubles as the laughs-per-minute telemetry every pitch says it will collect in playtests.

8. Distinctness was judged at the mechanics level; the feed judges it at the thumbnail. Only The Grand Delivery's silhouette (four unicorns roped to a giant screaming hot dog) passes on sight. Whichever concept wins should be specced from the ten-second clip backwards: what is on screen, who is screaming, what is recognizably this game.

9. Desktop-versus-phone asymmetry is treated as a rendering problem. The laptop player has a wider view and a keyboard; only The Grand Delivery's "spotter" line notices. Make the desktop player a role (map, radio, callouts) rather than pretending devices are equal.

10. Two-player sessions are all declared "complete" by scaling numbers, but the comedy of a two-person carry (R.E.P.O.'s piano) is a different comedy than four-person chaos, and nobody designed the two-player game as its own thing. The cart at 2 is the most credible of the six, and still unproven.
