## A. What research says makes games fun

**Researcher summary.** Research compiled from 11 bodies of work via web search (the sandbox proxy blocked direct page fetches, so details come from search-result abstracts and secondary summaries of the cited URLs; all URLs listed were seen in results). Strongest evidence: Self-Determination Theory (competence, autonomy, relatedness, intuitive controls predicting enjoyment and return play), cooperative+interdependent design raising relatedness/enjoyment/trust, endowed-progress and identification-through-customization effects, and the well-documented harms of loot-box/near-miss mechanics. Practitioner frameworks (Lazzaro's Four Keys, MDA, game feel/juice, systemic comedy) are widely used but less rigorously tested. Industry benchmarks put median mobile session length at 5-6 minutes and top-quartile D1 retention at ~26-33%, with most churn happening in the first session. For Glizzy Lovers: build a 3-6 minute co-op round vs. raccoons with interdependent roles, one-thumb controls, heavy juice, a 60-second character creator before the first match, deterministic (non-gambling) collections that start partially filled, and comedy that comes from physics and over-reacting NPCs rather than scripted jokes. Avoid competition-first design, appointment mechanics, forced accounts, and any randomized paid rewards.

### Principles

1. **Games are fun when they satisfy competence, autonomy and relatedness; these three needs independently predict enjoyment, preference and intention to keep playing (PENS model).** (confidence: high)
   - Design implication: Audit every 60-90 second slice of the core loop for all three: a legible competence hit (raccoon bonked, glizzy delivered, combo counter), a real choice (route, unicorn vs. on foot, which item to use), and a relatedness beat (assist credit, shared score pop, teammate emote). Round summaries should show 'what you did for the team', not just personal score.
   - Why it matters: Four studies (three lab, one MMO survey) found need satisfaction, not genre or graphics, predicted enjoyment, future play and short-term well-being; the 2010 follow-up review (Przybylski, Rigby & Ryan, Review of General Psychology) consolidated this as the dominant model of game motivation.
   - Source: Ryan, R. M., Rigby, C. S., & Przybylski, A. (2006). The motivational pull of video games: A self-determination theory approach. Motivation and Emotion, 30(4), 347-363. DOI 10.1007/s11031-006-9051-8 — https://acuresearchbank.acu.edu.au/item/8q128/the-motivational-pull-of-video-games-a-self-determination-theory-approach
2. **Intuitive controls are a precondition for need satisfaction and presence: when controls feel natural, players report more competence, autonomy and immersion.** (confidence: high)
   - Design implication: Mobile-first means one-thumb play: tap/hold/swipe, large hit targets, auto-aim or magnetism for throws, no virtual joystick if the design can avoid it, and sub-100ms input response. Test the control scheme on a phone with a friend in the room before adding any content; if a first-time player cannot do the core verb within 10 seconds without reading, redesign the verb, not the tutorial.
   - Why it matters: The paper found perceived intuitiveness of controls was associated with competence, autonomy and presence; on a phone browser, control friction is the single biggest threat to those feelings.
   - Source: Ryan, Rigby & Przybylski (2006), Studies 1-3 (intuitive controls subscale of PENS); PENS overview at selfdeterminationtheory.org — https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/player-experience-of-needs-satisfaction-pens/
3. **Cooperation and interdependence each independently increase relatedness, enjoyment and interpersonal trust, and their effects are additive.** (confidence: high)
   - Design implication: Do not ship 'parallel co-op' where friends merely play side by side. Give the room real interdependence: one player rides the unicorn while the other throws glizzies from the saddle, raccoon bosses need two players to shoo, shared health or a shared 'picnic basket' objective, and abilities that only work on a teammate (boost, revive, hat-return). Show both players' contributions in the same UI.
   - Why it matters: Controlled experiment using a Labyrinth-style puzzle game manipulating cooperation vs. competition and interdependence vs. independence; both factors raised relatedness, enjoyment and trust, and a game that is both cooperative and interdependent best builds and maintains friendships, which is the whole point of a 'play with friends' game.
   - Source: Depping, A. E., & Mandryk, R. L. (2017). Cooperation and Interdependence: How Multiplayer Games Increase Social Closeness. CHI PLAY 2017. — https://blogs.tuni.fi/playlab/game-research-highlights/how-to-design-games-that-promote-social-closeness-between-players/
4. **Cooperative play produces more empathy and prosocial behaviour than solo or competitive play; competitive modes raise positive affect but also aggression.** (confidence: medium)
   - Design implication: Make co-op vs. raccoons the default and only mandatory mode. Keep competition light and inside the team (most hot dogs saved, silliest fall) with friendly, self-deprecating framing; avoid direct PvP, ranked ladders, or anything that lets one friend make another feel bad. If you add versus, gate it behind a room-host toggle.
   - Why it matters: Emmerich & Masuch found empathy higher in the collaborative mode and positive affect and aggression higher in the competitive mode; Greitemeyer & Cox found pairs who played cooperatively showed more empathic concern afterward. For a light-hearted friend game, the co-op emotional profile is the one you want.
   - Source: Emmerich, K., & Masuch, M. (2013). Helping Friends or Fighting Foes: The Influence of Collaboration and Competition on Player Experience. FDG 2013 (n=58); Greitemeyer & Cox (2013) and Velez (2015) on co-op play and prosocial behaviour — https://www.depts.ttu.edu/comc/research/news/positive-effects-video-games.php
5. **Flow requires challenge matched to skill, clear goals and immediate feedback; in games the most robust way to hit it for mixed-skill groups is to let players adjust difficulty implicitly through their own choices rather than menus.** (confidence: high)
   - Design implication: Friend groups have wildly different skill, so widen the flow zone: raccoon waves scale with how far the group pushes into the map, optional 'spicy' zones with bigger rewards, rubber-banding for the weaker player (more assists, unicorn auto-pathing), and never interrupt a round with popups, ads or forced dialogs. Every round needs a visible goal (deliver 10 glizzies before the picnic ends) and instant feedback on every action.
   - Why it matters: Csikszentmihalyi's flow model is one of the most replicated constructs in positive psychology, and Chen's thesis/CACM article (and the game flOw) showed that embedding difficulty choices inside play keeps a broader range of players in the flow zone than explicit dynamic difficulty adjustment.
   - Source: Chen, J. (2007). Flow in games (and everything else). Communications of the ACM, 50(4), 31-34; Csikszentmihalyi, M. (1990). Flow: The Psychology of Optimal Experience. Harper & Row. — https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1145%2F1232743.1232769
6. **Endowed progress: people who receive artificial head-start progress toward a goal persist more and finish faster than people starting from zero.** (confidence: high)
   - Design implication: Never show an empty collection. Start the sticker book at 2/12, hand out the first outfit piece and first unicorn skin during the tutorial, pre-fill the first progress bar, and frame every collection as 'already begun'. Guest players should see this progress persist (localStorage) so creating an account later feels like 'saving what I already have', not starting over.
   - Why it matters: Field experiment: a car-wash loyalty card requiring 10 stamps with 2 pre-stamped was completed at almost double the rate and faster than an 8-stamp card starting empty, despite identical effort required.
   - Source: Nunes, J. C., & Drèze, X. (2006). The Endowed Progress Effect: How Artificial Advancement Increases Effort. Journal of Consumer Research, 32(4), 504-512. — https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/jconrs/v32y2006i4p504-512.html
7. **Avatar customization increases identification with the character, and higher identification sustains intrinsic motivation, effort and enjoyment over time.** (confidence: high)
   - Design implication: Put a short (about 60 seconds) but genuinely expressive character creator before the first match, available to guests without an account: body/face, a few silly hats, unicorn colour, a catchphrase emote. Make outfits visible to teammates in-lobby and in-round so expression is social. Keep customization deepening across sessions (new slots unlock) because identification compounds with time.
   - Why it matters: Birk et al. found players who identified less with their avatar showed faster waning of effort and enjoyment; Turkay & Kinzer's 66-participant, 10-hour LotRO study found both time and customization increased identification and that customizers were visibly more invested.
   - Source: Birk, M. V., Atkins, C., Bowey, J. T., & Mandryk, R. L. (2016). Fostering Intrinsic Motivation through Avatar Identification in Digital Games. CHI 2016, 2982-2995 (DOI 10.1145/2858036.2858062); Turkay, S., & Kinzer, C. K. (2014). The Effects of Avatar-Based Customization on Player Identification. IJGCMS. — https://www.irma-international.org/chapter/the-effects-of-avatar-based-customization-on-player-identification/126061
8. **Proteus effect: the look of a player's avatar changes how they behave, in-game and afterwards.** (confidence: high)
   - Design implication: Use appearance as gentle behavioural design: heroic or brave-looking outfits and big unicorns can nudge timid friends to take on the raccoon boss; comedic outfits license silliness. Offer confidence-signalling cosmetics as early rewards for shy or new players, and avoid cosmetics that visibly mark 'noob' status.
   - Why it matters: Participants assigned attractive avatars stood closer and disclosed more; taller avatars negotiated more aggressively, and a follow-up showed the effect carried into subsequent face-to-face interaction.
   - Source: Yee, N., & Bailenson, J. (2007). The Proteus Effect: The Effect of Transformed Self-Representation on Behavior. Human Communication Research, 33(3), 271-290. — https://vhil.stanford.edu/publications/avatars-and-agents/proteus-effect-implications-transformed-digital-self-representation
9. **Lazzaro's Four Keys: the most-loved games deliver at least three of Hard Fun (challenge, fiero), Easy Fun (curiosity, exploration, role-play), Serious Fun (relaxation, altered state) and People Fun (amusement, schadenfreude, naches from social play); People Fun is reported as the richest source of emotion.** (confidence: medium)
   - Design implication: Design explicitly for all four: Hard Fun via raccoon bosses and combo challenges; Easy Fun via a toy-like world (unicorn physics, things to poke, secrets); Serious Fun via a calm collection/decorate-your-cart mode; People Fun via emotes, a 'blame' button, ragdoll falls friends can laugh at, and reviving or teaching a newbie (naches). Playtest by counting laughs and out-loud reactions, not just completion.
   - Why it matters: Industry research with a small sample (n=30) rather than peer-reviewed, but widely adopted; secondary sources summarising her talks state there is more emotion in social interaction than in the other three keys combined, which I could not verify against the original PDF (fetch blocked).
   - Source: Lazzaro, N. (2004). Why We Play Games: Four Keys to More Emotion Without Story. XEODesign (field study of 30 adults using facial-expression coding). — https://www.researchgate.net/publication/248446107_Why_we_Play_Games_Four_Keys_to_More_Emotion_without_Story
10. **MDA: design from the aesthetics (the target feelings) backwards to dynamics and mechanics, choosing among eight kinds of fun (Sensation, Fantasy, Narrative, Challenge, Fellowship, Discovery, Expression, Submission).** (confidence: medium)
   - Design implication: Pick 3-4 target aesthetics for Glizzy Lovers (suggested: Fellowship, Expression, Sensation, Challenge, with Discovery as a secondary) and write them at the top of the design doc. Reject or rework any mechanic that does not serve one of them; e.g., outfits serve Expression, raccoon chaos serves Sensation, room codes serve Fellowship. Narrative only needs a premise and characters, not a plot.
   - Why it matters: Not empirical, but the most-cited formal framework in game design; it gives a shared vocabulary to keep a small team from adding features that dilute the intended feel.
   - Source: Hunicke, R., LeBlanc, M., & Zubek, R. (2004). MDA: A Formal Approach to Game Design and Game Research. AAAI Workshop on Challenges in Game AI. — https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~hunicke/MDA.pdf
11. **Quantic Foundry's data (hundreds of thousands of gamers): Community and Competition form one Social cluster and are positively correlated; Competition is the motivation that declines most with age; clear goals, feedback on progress, control over outcome, choices and self-expression (customizing a character) are near-universal wants.** (confidence: medium)
   - Design implication: Treat customization, clear goals and progress feedback as table stakes. Support both sides of the Social cluster: chat/emotes and team identity (Community) plus light in-team scoreboards (Competition), but if the audience is adult casual players, do not lead with competition. Consider a 'crew' identity (shared crew name and colours from the room code) to serve Community cheaply.
   - Why it matters: Very large self-report samples but not peer-reviewed; the age finding (r = -.30 for men, -.19 for women, steepest between 13 and 35) is the clearest evidence that a competition-first design narrows the audience.
   - Source: Yee, N. / Quantic Foundry. The Appeal of Competition Declines with Age (2016, n>239,000); GDC 2021 'Universals vs Differentiators' slides; Gamer Motivation Model (12 motivations, 400,000+ respondents) — https://quanticfoundry.com/2016/08/11/appeal-of-competition/
12. **Game feel and 'juice': abundant, immediate audiovisual feedback on every input (tweens, squash and stretch, particles, screenshake, sound) is what makes a mechanically identical game feel alive.** (confidence: medium)
   - Design implication: Budget real time for juice, not as polish at the end: every tap gets a sound and a visual reaction; hits have hit-stop and particles; unicorns bounce and stretch; raccoons yelp and fly. Use navigator.vibrate on Android for hits. Because it is co-op, make the other player's actions equally juicy on your screen. Provide a reduce-motion/screenshake toggle for accessibility.
   - Why it matters: Practitioner consensus rather than controlled evidence, but the live Breakout demo shows the effect starkly, and Swink's book ties feel to real-time control, simulated space and polish; on a phone browser with modest art, juice is the cheapest lever for a 'really well-made' feeling.
   - Source: Jonasson, M., & Purho, P. (2012). Juice it or lose it (talk, Nordic Game); Swink, S. (2008). Game Feel: A Game Designer's Guide to Virtual Sensation. Morgan Kaufmann. — https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Fy0aCDmgnxg
13. **Collections and completion drives are powerful, but variable-ratio rewards, engineered near-misses and paid loot boxes cross into gambling psychology and are linked to problem gambling.** (confidence: high)
   - Design implication: Make collections deterministic and transparent: earn tokens in rounds, spend them on the item you choose; every item shows exactly how to get it. If random drops exist, keep them free, cosmetic, with visible odds and a pity counter. Never sell randomized items for real money, never design 'almost got it' animations, and do not show players items they nearly unlocked as a nudge to spend.
   - Why it matters: Zendle & Cairns found loot-box spend correlated with problem-gambling severity more strongly than other in-game purchases; Drummond & Sauer found 45% of analysed games met all five psychological criteria for gambling; Clark et al. showed near-misses are experienced as less pleasant yet increase desire to play by recruiting win circuitry. A light-hearted friend game should not need any of it.
   - Source: Zendle, D., & Cairns, P. (2018). Video game loot boxes are linked to problem gambling. PLOS ONE (n=7,422); Drummond, A., & Sauer, J. D. (2018). Video game loot boxes are psychologically akin to gambling. Nature Human Behaviour; Clark, L. et al. (2009). Gambling near-misses enhance motivation to gamble and recruit win-related brain circuitry. Neuron. — https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/139116/1/journal.pone.0206767.pdf
14. **Comedy in games works best when it is systemic: simple player verbs, exaggerated NPC reactions and physics produce fresh, player-authored jokes, whereas scripted gags wear out on repeat.** (confidence: medium)
   - Design implication: Make raccoons the comedy engine: give them a small set of over-the-top reactions (panic, tantrum, steal-and-taunt, dramatic faint) that fire off systemic triggers, and reward pranks done when the raccoon is not looking. Give the unicorn slightly unruly physics so movement itself is funny (Octodad's lesson). Keep dialogue minimal and cartoon-like. Track laughs in playtests as a primary metric.
   - Why it matters: Dormann & Biddle's review frames humor's social, emotional and cognitive functions for design; the Goose and Octodad teams describe concretely how antagonistic relationships plus over-reaction and awkward controls produce reliable laughs without writing jokes, which also scales better for a small team.
   - Source: House House interviews on Untitled Goose Game (Disseldorp: 'immersive slapstick sim', pranks funnier when victims aren't looking, Hitman-style stealth systems); Young Horses, 'There and Dad Again' Octodad postmortem (GDC Next 2014); Dormann, C., & Biddle, R. (2009). A Review of Humor for Computer Games: Play, Laugh and More. Simulation & Gaming, 40(6), 802-824. — https://www.vice.com/en/article/honks-vs-quacks-a-long-chat-with-the-developers-of-untitled-goose-game/
15. **The first session decides retention: most players who churn do so within the first day, and top mobile games reach core fun within the first minutes and keep sessions short.** (confidence: medium)
   - Design implication: From tap to controlling your character should take under 30 seconds (no account, no long load, room code optional). Teach in play: the first raccoon cannot beat you. Design rounds of 3-6 minutes so a whole match fits the median session (5-6 min) with a natural 'one more' hook. Instrument tutorial completion (target 70-80%), time-to-first-glizzy, first-session length, D1/D7, and watch where first sessions end.
   - Why it matters: Industry telemetry rather than controlled studies, and it covers native apps not browser games, but the direction is consistent across GameAnalytics, devtodev, Unity and Deconstructor of Fun: players form their opinion in the first minutes and the majority never come back after session one.
   - Source: GameAnalytics 2025 Mobile Gaming Benchmarks (11,600 games, 1.48B MAU): top-25% D1 26-28% (iOS 31-33%), bottom-25% 10-11.5%; top-25% session length 8-9 min, median 5-6 min; devtodev, How to Analyze First Player Session (tutorial completion target 70-80%); Unity FTUE tips — https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gameanalytics-mobile-gaming-benchmarks
16. **Failure is the engine of fun only when it is cheap, quick to recover from and emotionally safe; in a light-hearted game failure should be funny, not punishing.** (confidence: medium)
   - Design implication: Make losing a punchline: raccoons steal your hat and wear it, the unicorn faceplants, the picnic gets 'slightly ruined'. Instant retry, teammate revives, no lives, energy or lockout timers, and no loss of collected items. Show a funny 'blooper' stat at the end of failed rounds so the group still gets a shared moment.
   - Why it matters: Juul argues players return to escape the feeling of inadequacy that failure creates; in a co-op comedy the safer route is to let failure itself produce amusement (People Fun) rather than fiero-through-grind.
   - Source: Juul, J. (2013). The Art of Failure: An Essay on the Pain of Playing Video Games. MIT Press. — https://thevideogamelibrary.org/book/the-art-of-failure-an-essay-on-the-pain-of-playing-video-games
17. **Dark patterns (temporal, monetary, social) demonstrably harm players and now attract regulatory penalties; a game 'for friends' loses trust fastest when it exploits them.** (confidence: high)
   - Design implication: Explicitly ban: appointment mechanics that punish absence, grind walls with pay-to-skip, pay-to-win, 'invite friends to unlock' pyramids, disguised long-term costs, and confusing purchase buttons. Optional accounts must stay optional (no nag screens, no features held hostage). If you monetize, sell clearly-priced cosmetics with a confirm step and no randomization.
   - Why it matters: Zagal et al. define dark game design patterns as intentionally creating negative experiences against players' interests without consent (grinding, appointment play, pay-to-win, social pyramids); the FTC found Fortnite's counterintuitive button layout caused unwanted purchases, showing these patterns are now enforced against, not just frowned on.
   - Source: Zagal, J., Björk, S., & Lewis, C. (2013). Dark Patterns in the Design of Games. FDG 2013; King, D. L., & Delfabbro, P. H. (2018). Predatory monetization schemes in video games. Addiction; FTC v. Epic Games settlement (Dec 2022, $245M refunds for dark patterns) — https://research.chalmers.se/en/publication/177148

### Pitfalls

- Forcing account creation or a long tutorial before play: most churn happens in the first session, and industry sources (devtodev, Unity, GameAnalytics) agree that time-to-core-fun in the first minutes is the biggest retention lever. Let guests play with a room code immediately; offer the account only after the first win, framed as 'save your hat'.
- Competition-first or PvP-first design: Quantic Foundry data shows Competition is the motivation that declines most with age (steepest 13-35) and is the largest gender gap among young players; a friend-group game should default to co-op vs. raccoons with only light in-team comparison.
- Parallel co-op with no interdependence: Depping & Mandryk found interdependence is an independent driver of relatedness, enjoyment and trust; two people playing next to each other without needing each other forfeits most of the social payoff.
- Loot boxes, paid random rewards, or engineered near-misses: linked to problem-gambling severity (Zendle & Cairns 2018), meet gambling criteria (Drummond & Sauer 2018), and near-misses increase urge to play while feeling worse (Clark et al. 2009). Keep collections deterministic and transparent.
- Dark patterns (appointment mechanics, grind walls with pay-to-skip, social pyramid invites, confusing purchase flows, hidden long-term costs): defined by Zagal, Björk & Lewis (2013) and King & Delfabbro (2018), and now penalized by regulators (FTC v. Epic Games, 2022).
- Interrupting flow with popups, ads, mid-round tutorials or forced dialogs: flow theory (Csikszentmihalyi; Chen 2007) depends on uninterrupted challenge-skill matching with immediate feedback.
- Fixed difficulty tuned for one skill level: mixed-skill friend groups need a wide flow zone (implicit difficulty choice, rubber-banding, assists) or the weaker player churns and the stronger one is bored.
- Virtual joysticks and multi-finger controls on phone browsers: intuitive controls predict competence, autonomy and presence (Ryan et al. 2006); clumsy touch controls undermine everything above.
- Scripted, repeating jokes: comedy that fires the same line every time wears out; Goose Game and Octodad show that systemic reactions and physics produce fresh, player-authored laughs.
- Punishing failure (lives, lockout timers, losing collected items): in a light-hearted game failure should be a punchline and a shared moment, not a reason to stop playing (Juul 2013; Lazzaro's People Fun).
- Over-juicing without accessibility toggles: heavy screenshake and flashing can cause discomfort; the same practitioner community that popularized 'juice' warns about restraint (Game Developer: 'indies, resist the urge to juice it or lose it'). Ship reduce-motion and shake-off options.
- Empty collections and zero-progress starts: the endowed progress effect shows partially completed goals are pursued harder; a 0/50 sticker book is demotivating.
- Promising a fantasy the game does not deliver: Deconstructor of Fun notes many early retention failures stem from a mismatch between the fantasy that attracted players and the actual first-session experience; the store page, title screen and first round should all be 'ride a unicorn, save hot dogs from raccoons, with your friends'.
- Weak or absent per-player expression: self-expression via character customization is a near-universal want (Quantic Foundry) and drives identification and sustained motivation (Birk et al. 2016); shipping without a creator or with cosmetics locked behind long grinds hurts retention.

### Researcher's open questions

- Full texts could not be fetched (the sandbox egress proxy blocked every page fetch), so all details come from search-result abstracts and secondary summaries of the listed URLs; direct quotes and exact figures should be re-checked against the primary sources before being printed in any public design doc.
- Lazzaro's claim that People Fun yields more emotion than the other three keys combined came from secondary sources summarising her talks (e.g., Yu-kai Chou's write-up, Voices of VR); the exact wording in the 2004 XEODesign paper was not verified.
- Emmerich & Masuch (2017) 'The Impact of Game Patterns on Player Experience and Social Interaction in Co-Located Multiplayer Games' (CHI PLAY 2017, pp. 411-422) was confirmed to exist but its specific findings could not be retrieved; the co-op principle relies on their 2013 FDG study and Depping & Mandryk instead.
- No meta-analysis specifically on cooperative play and enjoyment/relatedness was found; the meta-analytic evidence located concerns violent vs. prosocial game content (Greitemeyer & Mügge 2014) rather than co-op mode per se.
- Session-length and retention benchmarks (GameAnalytics 2025) describe native iOS/Android apps; comparable public benchmarks for mobile web/browser games were not found, and browser games likely have shorter sessions and lower D1 given no home-screen icon.
- The 'players decide in the first 2-3 minutes' and '20% abandon within two minutes' figures appear only in vendor/analytics blogs (scoopanalytics, robosoft) with no disclosed methodology; treat as directional, low confidence.
- Story: the research does not call for much. Lazzaro's paper is literally titled 'more emotion without story', MDA lists Narrative as only one of eight aesthetics, and the comedy sources favour minimal dialogue. From the user, the useful inputs are: the tone (how absurd vs. cozy), the world premise in 2-3 sentences (why are raccoons after the glizzies, what are unicorns to these people), 3-5 named characters or raccoon archetypes with one trait each, and a list of 10-20 slapstick gags they find funny. A plot is not needed for v1.

## B. Friendslop genre and browser party games

**Researcher summary.** METHOD NOTE. The sandbox egress proxy blocked WebFetch on every domain except github.com, so every fact below comes from search-result excerpts of the cited pages (page titles and URLs were confirmed in results) plus one GitHub README fetch. Numbers supported by a single excerpt are marked medium; anything I could not corroborate was dropped or marked low. No source, author, date or URL below is invented.

=== A. FRIENDSLOP ===

DEFINITION AND ORIGIN
- Wikipedia's lead (as excerpted): "a slang word used to describe a subgenre of cooperative indie games oriented around a low barrier of entry and social interaction, emphasizing gameplay in social groups... low-budget, low-cost indie games intended to attract entire friend groups as an online social activity" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friendslop).
- Know Your Meme: earliest use Nov 16 2024 by @regularaugust about slice-of-life anime (unrelated); the gaming meaning starts Mar 17 2025 with @wooosaaaahhhhh naming Lethal Company, Content Warning and R.E.P.O.; the poster clarified it was a joke, and the post was mocked with "anyone using the term surely has no friends" replies (https://knowyourmeme.com/editorials/guides/whats-the-term-for-games-like-lethal-company-and-repo-people-calling-co-op-games-friendslop-explained).
- Aftermath, Jay Castello, Jul 17 2025, "'Friendslop' Isn't Real, But People Love Posting About It Anyway": argues the term "said genuinely" never existed; it is a social-media dunk cycle (https://aftermath.site/friendslop-peak-lethal-company-social-media-discourse).
- AV Club, Bee Wertheimer, Jul 10 2025, "Not Every Co-Op Game Is 'Friendslop'": Lethal Company "spearheaded the co-op craze"; many follow-ups were "rushed out to cash in on the trend" (https://www.avclub.com/not-every-co-op-game-is-friendslop).
- Creative Bloq (2026): "low-cost, indie-made multiplayer titles built around simple mechanics and chaotic group play... the fun comes almost entirely from the people you play with" (https://creativebloq.com/3d/video-game-design/what-is-friendslop-and-why-it-it-taking-over-gaming-in-2026).
- The label is now embraced by developers: Aggro Crab's Nick Kaman at GDC (to PC Gamer's Ted Litchfield): "Why not make a game where the point is to hang out with your friends? ... These are games that just focus on that and make mechanical choices that encourage that behavior... It's technically derogatory. But why not lean into that?" (https://www.inkl.com/news/peak-boss-doesnt-mind-us-calling-it-friendslop-why-not-make-a-game-where-the-point-is-to-hang-out-with-your-friends). Steam even hosts a "Friendslop Games" franchise page and a game literally titled Friendslop (https://store.steampowered.com/franchise/friendslop-games, https://store.steampowered.com/app/5019410/Friendslop/).

TOP-5 PROFILES
1) LETHAL COMPANY (Zeekerss, solo; Early Access Oct 23 2023; ~$9.99; up to 4 players). Loop: land on abandoned moons, scavenge scrap, sell to "The Company" to meet a rising profit quota. Each moon day is ~11 real minutes (8am to midnight, then the ship leaves); each quota cycle gives 3 days, so a cycle is ~33 minutes; missing quota gets the crew jettisoned (https://lethal-company.fandom.com/wiki/Profit_Quota, https://uhsarrow.org/28857/opinion/lethal-company-literal-deadlines-in-space). CRITIQUES AND WHAT SEPARATES THE LASTING ONES. AppMagic's 2025 analysis: "Most players are gone within a few days"; these games "aim to own a handful of evenings"; "30-day retention hovers around 3%"; "Meta-progression barely exists... almost all progress happens inside a single run"; "one or two friends is the bare minimum, and a full squad of four is where the fun actually starts" (https://appmagic.rocks/blog/friendslop-steam-games-2025). The ones that keep a base do it with big named updates that re-spike CCU (R.E.P.O.), a multi-year roadmap (Phasmophobia), or a mod ecosystem (Lethal Company); PEAK's devs openly declined the live-service path.

=== "FRIENDSLOP FEEL" CHECKLIST (test a Glizzy Lovers concept against each) ===
1. Zero-tutorial start: can 3–4 strangers to the game be laughing inside 5 minutes with nothing but a friend explaining? (Lethal Company; Gartic Phone)
2. Cheap, frequent, visible failure: does dying/dropping/falling cost almost nothing and happen in front of teammates? ("laughing at death")
3. One external pressure: is there a quota, timer or faceless boss forcing greedy decisions? (Company quota, Taxman, SpookTube views, destruction quota)
4. Shared physical burden: must the group jointly move/carry/hold something whose physics punish poor coordination? (R.E.P.O. hauling, Chained Together chain, PEAK rope)
5. Floppy bodies: do characters and enemies ragdoll or wobble so mistakes read as slapstick? (flappy heads, "retro jank")
6. Spatial voice or an expressive stand-in: does distance change what teammates hear/see, so separation creates drama? (proximity chat)
7. Bounded run: is a full cycle 20–45 minutes with a hard end and a "one more" continuation? (3 days ≈ 33 min; 3-night rounds; Jackbox 15–20)
8. Clip test: does an average session yield at least one 10-second moment a streamer would post? (Among Us on Twitch; Meccha Chameleon)
9. One code to join: no download, no account, no reading; price trivial or free. (Jackbox, skribbl, Gartic, Codenames; $5–10 Steam norm)
10. Satirical bad job: is the framing a dystopian employer or gig you are bad at? (Company, SpookTube, Taxman, world's worst airline)
11. Works at 2, sings at 4: is the design tuned for a full squad without breaking at 2? (AppMagic)
12. Light meta: is progression mostly inside a run, with cosmetics not power outside it? (AppMagic; cosmetic-only norms)
13. Absurd over scary: are the "baddies" funnier than they are frightening? (raccoons fit; genre swings "between terrifying silence and laugh-out-loud disasters")
14. Bad players still matter: can a low-skill player contribute via timing, talking or holding, not reflexes? (Jackbox asynchronous design)

STORY YOU NEED FROM THE USER (implied by the evidence): the genre runs on a one-paragraph premise, a faceless employer/boss, and a quota, not a plot; Lethal Company, Content Warning, R.E.P.O. and Yapyap each ship exactly that.

### Principles

1. **COPY: Make death and failure the expected outcome so mishaps become the joke rather than a setback.** (confidence: high)
   - Design implication: Glizzy Lovers should respawn instantly, keep run progress on death, and broadcast each wipeout to the whole group (ragdoll, sound, teammate camera). Never make a raccoon ambush cost meta-progress.
   - Why it matters: Lethal Company's whole comedic register depends on the fear of losing progress being absent; players already expect to die.
   - Source: Push to Talk interview with Zeekerss ("a game about laughing at death") and Naavik takeaways — https://www.pushtotalk.gg/p/how-lethal-company-sold-10-million-copies
2. **COPY: Voice that changes with distance (proximity chat) is the genre's primary comedy engine.** (confidence: high)
   - Design implication: Design the map so groups split up and reunite; if browser voice is not viable on mobile Safari, ship a spatial stand-in (distance-attenuated emotes, walkie-talkie items, shout radius) and assume players are on Discord/FaceTime.
   - Why it matters: Every canonical friendslop title (Lethal Company, Content Warning, R.E.P.O., PEAK) leans on voice-driven emergent moments.
   - Source: Naavik, Lethal Company takeaways; Windows Central review describing hearing crewmates screaming down corridors — https://naavik.co/digest/lethal-company-takeaways/
3. **COPY: A hard quota plus a short fixed day timer shapes both risk-taking and session length.** (confidence: high)
   - Design implication: Use a visible Boss/quota (e.g. glizzies delivered per shift) and an ~10-minute shift timer; three shifts per quota gives a ~30-minute run that ends decisively, matching Lethal Company's 11-minute days and 3-day cycles.
   - Why it matters: Deadline pressure is what turns a sandbox into stories ("we had 40 seconds left and Dave was carrying the whole haul").
   - Source: Lethal Company Wiki, Profit Quota; UHS Arrow, "Lethal Company: literal deadlines in space" — https://lethal-company.fandom.com/wiki/Profit_Quota
4. **COPY: Floppy physics bodies are a deliberate comedy choice, discovered by playtesting, not polish.** (confidence: medium)
   - Design implication: Give players and unicorn mounts wobbly, over-reactive physics (flappy heads, loose necks, bouncy saddles) and let items tumble; test in a group before tuning realism away.
   - Why it matters: R.E.P.O.'s identity came from a physics gag; PC Gamer names "ragdolls" as a defining trope of the genre.
   - Source: Semiwork co-CEO Walter Tischkewitz: "We just played around with that and we realized this is pretty funny" — https://tech.yahoo.com/gaming/articles/lets-just-fail-quickly-repo-142853084.html
5. **COPY: Give the group a shared physical burden that forces coordination talk.** (confidence: medium)
   - Design implication: Make the big prizes two- or four-person carries (a giant glizzy, a cart, a chained sausage link) with physics that punish uncoordinated movement; on touch, use drag-and-hold gestures rather than button combos.
   - Why it matters: Semiwork said their earlier prototype failed because "nothing was keeping players together"; the shared object fixed that.
   - Source: Co-Optimus on R.E.P.O.: physics hauling gives "a real reason for players to keep talking, keep coordinating" — https://www.co-optimus.com/blog/article-poster/3380/the-game-where-a-piano-is-scarier-than-the-monsters.html
6. **COPY: Make mechanical choices whose explicit purpose is hanging out with friends.** (confidence: high)
   - Design implication: Prefer mechanics that require talking (calling out raccoons, negotiating who rides the unicorn) over parallel solo tasks; every system should be justified by "does this make the group talk?"
   - Why it matters: PEAK's studio head frames this as the whole design brief of the genre.
   - Source: Nick Kaman (Aggro Crab) to PC Gamer at GDC, via inkl — https://www.inkl.com/news/peak-boss-doesnt-mind-us-calling-it-friendslop-why-not-make-a-game-where-the-point-is-to-hang-out-with-your-friends
7. **COPY: Bounded runs with a clear ending and an optional continuation (three days, three nights).** (confidence: high)
   - Design implication: Structure play as runs of ~3 shifts; success unlocks "keep the job" continuation, failure ends the run with a comedic firing screen. Fits a browser session and a 15–20 minute party-game norm.
   - Why it matters: Every 2024–2026 hit uses the same small-run cadence; Yapyap copies it (three nights per round).
   - Source: Gematsu on Content Warning (three-day runs, keep going if views suffice); Netflix Tudum on Jackbox game length — https://gematsu.com/2024/04/landfall-releases-four-player-co-op-horror-game-content-warning-for-pc
8. **COPY: Teach through peers and dying, but keep the ruleset legible enough to learn in one short run.** (confidence: medium)
   - Design implication: No tutorial screens; instead a first shift with slower raccoons, on-object hints, and a visible goal. Avoid vagueness that needs many runs to decode.
   - Why it matters: The genre's onboarding is social, but the developer himself found pure vagueness fails in short-format games.
   - Source: Destructoid on Lethal Company's lack of tutorial; Zeekerss in Steam discussion admitting the vague design was "too short to allow people to learn" — https://destructoid.com/?p=433008
9. **COPY: Launch cheap or free-first to flood feeds with footage.** (confidence: high)
   - Design implication: Glizzy Lovers should be free to play with a shareable room link; consider a free launch window or invite-a-friend-free mechanic so the first day produces clips from many groups at once.
   - Why it matters: Content Warning's 6.6M free claims produced the wave that later sold 1M paid copies.
   - Source: Landfall CEO Wilhelm Nylund via Neowin: people "woke up one day and suddenly they're just bombarded with a bunch of footage from a new game" — https://www.neowin.net/news/content-warning-still-sold-1-million-copies-on-steam-after-it-gave-away-66-million-for-free/
10. **COPY: Build for streamers and clips; the genre spreads through watched reactions.** (confidence: high)
   - Design implication: Add an in-game clip/screenshot button, spectator view for eliminated players, and a share-to-social summary card per run; keep faces/voices central to what is on screen.
   - Why it matters: Among Us (2018) was dead until streamers played it in July 2020; Meccha Chameleon's 20M sales are attributed to clip-friendly moments.
   - Source: Kotaku on Among Us's Twitch rise: it "gives off the same atmosphere as a Jackbox Games session... which translates really well to Twitch" — https://kotaku.com/among-us-improbable-rise-to-the-top-of-twitch-1844986025
11. **COPY: Short room code plus a display name is the entire join flow; no app, no account.** (confidence: high)
   - Design implication: Host gets a 4-letter code and a link; joiner types code and a name (blank name gets a random glizzy pun) and is in the lobby in under 10 seconds.
   - Why it matters: Jackbox, skribbl.io, Gartic Phone, JKLM.fun and Codenames all use this pattern; it is the browser party-game standard.
   - Source: Built In on Jackbox: punch in a shared room code on a web page, no app, no peripherals — https://builtin.com/media-gaming/jackbox-games-design-party-pack
12. **COPY: Treat the phone as a limited, private controller: hidden info and timed choices, not a cramped joystick.** (confidence: high)
   - Design implication: Use per-player private prompts (secret raccoon sightings, individual orders) and timed decisions; keep continuous movement simple so a phone does not need dozens of buttons.
   - Why it matters: It turns the phone's weakness (no physical controls) into a design strength.
   - Source: Built In: "you can't feasibly cram a dozen buttons and replicate a joystick on an iPhone"; per-phone private information enables hidden-information mechanics — https://www.builtinchicago.org/articles/jackbox-games-design-party-pack
13. **COPY: Guest-first identity with a later, motivated upgrade to a username/password account.** (confidence: high)
   - Design implication: Create an anonymous profile on first play (device-bound), then offer account creation when the player earns their first outfit or wants progress on a second device; never gate the lobby behind sign-up.
   - Why it matters: Bloxd.io, skribbl.io, Gartic Phone and Codenames all play without an account; Bloxd added optional accounts later for custom names.
   - Source: Unity Authentication docs on anonymous auth and linking — https://docs.unity.com/en-us/authentication/anonymous-auth-and-linking
14. **COPY: Offer two touch movement schemes (virtual joystick with a size slider, or tap/hold-to-move) and a dynamically positioned joystick.** (confidence: medium)
   - Design implication: Ship both schemes in settings; spawn the joystick under the first touch in the lower-left zone and limit its active area so it never overlaps action buttons.
   - Why it matters: Among Us, the largest mobile social game, found one scheme was not enough; fixed-position sticks cause unintended movement.
   - Source: Everyeye guide to Among Us mobile controls (Joystick vs Touch); Phaser virtual joystick write-up on dynamic repositioning and zone limiting — https://phaser.io/news/2025/12/i-built-the-best-virtual-joystick-for-phaserjs
15. **COPY: Sessions of minutes, matches of 15–20 minutes.** (confidence: high)
   - Design implication: Target one shift in ~8–10 minutes and a full run in ~30; allow drop-in/drop-out so a phone player who takes a call can rejoin.
   - Why it matters: Mobile browser sessions are interrupt-prone; short bounded rounds survive interruptions.
   - Source: Netflix Tudum on Jackbox (15–20 minutes per game); God is a Geek on Stumble Guys three-round structure — https://netflix.com/tudum/articles/jackbox-games-party-essentials-news
16. **COPY: Monetize cosmetics only; no gameplay advantage for money.** (confidence: medium)
   - Design implication: Outfits, hats, unicorn skins and emotes are the store; collections and outfit sets are earned in runs or bought, but stats never change.
   - Why it matters: The social-game audience tolerates hats, not pay-to-win; Among Us monetized 217M installs on hats and pets.
   - Source: GameDiscoverCo Shell Shockers deep dive (cosmetics give no advantage); Windows Central on Among Us free vs paid — https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/deep-dive-shell-shockers-multi-million
17. **COPY: If using ads, place them at natural breaks (death, between shifts) and sell a VIP that removes them.** (confidence: medium)
   - Design implication: Use a Poki-style commercialBreak between shifts and rewarded ads for a bonus glizzy; offer a cheap 'Union Card' VIP that removes ads and adds cosmetics.

   - Source: GameDiscoverCo: Shell Shockers is 80–90% ad-driven, VIP removes ads, high death rate makes ads non-intrusive — https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/deep-dive-shell-shockers-multi-million
18. **COPY: Daily missions feeding a free/premium pass are the mobile-standard return hook; keep them light.** (confidence: high)
   - Design implication: Three daily 'shift goals' (e.g. deliver 10 glizzies, ride a unicorn for 60s) that pay outfit currency; a seasonal free track with an optional paid track. Avoid heavy grind that contradicts the genre's light meta.
   - Why it matters: Stumble Guys reached 270M downloads and $80M+ with this loop; Krunker uses daily challenges paying KR for skins.

19. **COPY: Ground customization in self-determination needs: outfits/collections serve autonomy, co-op serves relatedness, clear skill growth serves competence.** (confidence: high)
   - Design implication: Player creation and outfit collections are not fluff; expose them early (first outfit unlock in run one), and make competence visible via per-run stats and silly titles.

   - Why it matters: Room-code games live or die on whether the two friends who showed up can still have a good time.
   - Source: AppMagic: "one or two friends is the bare minimum, and a full squad of four is where the fun actually starts"; PCGamesN on Supermarket Together scaling demand with players — https://appmagic.rocks/blog/friendslop-steam-games-2025

   - Why it matters: These portals are the largest free distribution channels for browser games (CrazyGames ~78M visits in Dec 2025).

26. **COPY: Cross-play via one room code across desktop and phone browsers.** (confidence: high)
   - Design implication: A single code works whether the host is on a laptop or a phone; the game must render both layouts from the same session state.
   - Why it matters: Friend groups are mixed-device; forcing everyone onto the same device type shrinks the lobby.

   - Why it matters: With ~3% 30-day retention, designing the game to be finishable is more honest than a treadmill.

   - Design implication: The first screen is Join/Host; profile creation is optional and appears after the first run.
   - Why it matters: The entire browser party-game category converges on zero-friction joining; the user's 'optional profile' requirement matches it.
   - Source: KYM on Gartic Phone (browser, no sign-ups); Bloxd.io history (accounts added later, optional) — https://knowyourmeme.com/editorials/guides/what-is-gartic-phone-and-why-do-streamers-love-it-the-multiplayer-drawing-game-explained
30. **AVOID: Don't make reflex precision the skill gate on phones.** (confidence: medium)
   - Design implication: Prefer timing windows, holding, steering and talking over twitch aiming; let a bad player still be the one who holds the door or distracts the raccoon.
   - Why it matters: Touch latency and small screens punish reflex play; social games are won on inclusion.

31. **AVOID: Don't rely on browser WebRTC voice as the only comedy channel on mobile Safari.** (confidence: high)
   - Design implication: Ship spatial emotes, quick-chat and horns as first-class; treat in-browser voice as optional and expect Discord/FaceTime; if implementing voice, gate on a tap and handle muted tracks by re-acquiring getUserMedia.
   - Why it matters: Proximity chat is the genre's engine, but Safari mutes tracks with no unmute API and blocks new audio elements in background tabs.

32. **AVOID: Don't assume the WebSocket survives a backgrounded iOS Safari tab.** (confidence: high)
   - Design implication: On visibilitychange to hidden, flush and close; on foreground, reconnect immediately and request a full state sync; make the server keep the seat for ~2 minutes so the player rejoins the same run.
   - Why it matters: Phone players switch apps mid-run constantly; a drop that kills the run breaks the social contract.
   - Source: Apple Developer Forums thread on iOS 15 Safari WebSocket closing when backgrounded; omr.it.com on 30-second background suspension and reconnect-with-sync strategy — https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/696310
33. **AVOID: Don't use 100vh or the Fullscreen API for the game viewport on iOS.** (confidence: high)

   - Why it matters: A clipped bottom bar or a fullscreen that exits when the player swipes makes the joystick unusable.
   - Source: itch.io post by ocias on iPad fullscreen caveats; Nicolas Hoizey on viewport height exceeding the visible area — https://itch.io/profile/ocias
34. **AVOID: Don't leave default touch gestures enabled on the play surface.** (confidence: high)
   - Design implication: Set touch-action: none on the game canvas (or manipulation on UI), disable double-tap zoom and pull-to-refresh, and keep the screen awake with the Screen Wake Lock API during a run.
   - Why it matters: Zoom-on-double-tap and dimming screens are the most common ways a mobile web game silently breaks.
   - Source: Codrops CSS reference on touch-action (via chenhuijing); W3C web roadmap on Screen Wake Lock and Vibration APIs (GitHub, fetched) — https://github.com/w3c/web-roadmaps/blob/51573bae79b10b9733c1ab92cc790db185d8c5e7/mobile/userinput.html
35. **AVOID: Don't play any audio before the first user gesture, and don't assume many simultaneous audio elements.** (confidence: medium)
   - Design implication: Unlock a single AudioContext on the Join tap; mix SFX through Web Audio rather than many <audio> tags; show a mute toggle on the lobby.
   - Why it matters: Silent games feel broken; blocked autoplay is the top reason browser games start mute on iOS.
   - Source: W3C CoreMob quality-of-implementation notes on mobile audio limitations — https://www.w3.org/community/coremob/wiki/Quality_of_Implementation/Audio.html
36. **AVOID: Don't call third-party APIs directly from the game client if you want portal or Discord distribution.** (confidence: high)
   - Design implication: Proxy everything through your own origin (or Discord URL mappings); Poki blocks external requests by default and Discord's CSP returns blocked:csp for unmapped hosts.
   - Why it matters: An auth or analytics call to an external host will fail silently inside these embeds.
   - Source: Discord networking guide on CSP and URL mappings; Poki SDK requirements — https://docs.discord.com/developers/activities/development-guides/networking
37. **AVOID: Don't treat mobile browser as a scaled-down desktop build.** (confidence: medium)
   - Design implication: Design the HUD, joystick zones and text sizes for phones first (CrazyGames requires legible text on mobile); test on real iOS Safari and Android Chrome, not only devtools emulation.
   - Why it matters: Bloxd.io is a cautionary example: playable on mobile browsers but with a control experience reported as rougher than desktop.
   - Source: CrazyGames technical requirements (text and images must be legible on mobile; responsive 16:9 iframe) — https://docs.crazygames.com/requirements/technical

### Pitfalls

- Genre churn is structural, not a bug: AppMagic reports most players gone within days and ~3% 30-day retention; do not promise investors or yourself Phasmophobia-style longevity without a roadmap budget (https://appmagic.rocks/blog/friendslop-steam-games-2025).
- 'Fun depends on your friends' cuts both ways: Creative Bloq notes the fun 'comes almost entirely from the people you play with', so a solo or 2-player first session that feels empty will lose the player before they invite anyone (https://creativebloq.com/3d/video-game-design/what-is-friendslop-and-why-it-it-taking-over-gaming-in-2026).
- Trope-only clones get dismissed as cash-ins (AV Club); every breakout had one new physical verb (https://www.avclub.com/not-every-co-op-game-is-friendslop).
- Vagueness as onboarding fails in short formats; Zeekerss himself said the original vague design was 'too short to allow people to learn' (https://steamcommunity.com/app/1966720/discussions/0/4141689926404770481/?ctp=2).
- iOS Safari closes WebSockets when the tab is backgrounded (since iOS 15) and onclose may never fire after long inactivity; without reconnect-plus-state-sync, phone players lose their run (https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/696310).
- Fullscreen API is unusable on iPhone and hostile on iPad (swipe exits, repeated taps trigger a phishing warning); 100vh clips the bottom of the layout under the toolbar (https://itch.io/profile/ocias, https://archives.nicolas-hoizey.com/2001-gasteroprod/web/viewport-height-is-taller-than-the-visible-part-of-the-document-in-some-mobile-browsers.html).
- In-browser voice on mobile Safari: getUserMedia ducks or pauses other audio, captured tracks can be muted with no unmute API, and new audio elements cannot be created while the tab is in the background (https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=218815, https://webrtchacks.com/autoplay-restrictions-and-webrtc/).
- Audio autoplay is blocked until a user gesture and some mobile browsers cap simultaneous audio elements (https://www.w3.org/community/coremob/wiki/Quality_of_Implementation/Audio.html).
- Default touch gestures (double-tap zoom, pinch, pull-to-refresh) hijack joystick input unless touch-action is set; screens dim mid-run without a wake lock (https://chenhuijing.com/blog/codrops-css-reference-touch-action/, https://github.com/w3c/web-roadmaps/blob/51573bae79b10b9733c1ab92cc790db185d8c5e7/mobile/userinput.html).
- localStorage throws in incognito/private mode; Poki requires try/catch around it (https://sdk.poki.com/requirements).
- Portal and Discord embeds block direct external requests (Poki default block; Discord CSP blocked:csp), so auth/analytics/voice SDKs must be proxied through your origin (https://docs.discord.com/developers/activities/development-guides/networking).
- Virtual joysticks with fixed positions cause unintended movement; Among Us needed two control schemes and a size slider to satisfy players (https://discussions.unity.com/t/opinions-wanted-why-are-joystick-controls-so-terrible-on-mobile/640042, https://www.everyeye.it/notizie/among-us-come-gioca-telefono-guida-differenze-pc-477920.html).
- Bloxd.io shows the cost of desktop-first: playable in mobile browsers but with a control experience reported as rougher than desktop (https://querygame.substack.com/p/unveiling-bloxdio-a-voxelated-adventure).
- Payload size is a hard gate on portals: Poki asks for an initial download under 8 MB, CrazyGames under 20 MB for mobile homepage eligibility (https://sdk.poki.com/requirements, https://docs.crazygames.com/requirements/technical).
- Discord Activity access can be gated by Discord itself: Gartic Phone's activity became Nitro-only weeks after launch, so do not make Discord the sole distribution channel (https://game.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/news/1486253.html).
- Sourcing caveat: page fetches were blocked for all hosts except github.com, so quotes and figures are from search excerpts; figures from single excerpts (PEAK 10M, Chained Together sales, Krunker early DAU, portrait-share statistics) should be re-verified before being repeated externally.

### Researcher's open questions

- Voice strategy: is Glizzy Lovers designed around in-browser proximity voice (high technical risk on iOS Safari) or around an external voice channel plus expressive in-game stand-ins? This decision shapes the whole comedy design.
- Orientation: portrait favors one-handed casual play and is reported as the majority of mobile releases (single weak source), while every friendslop title is a landscape 3D game; a physics co-op game likely needs landscape, but Poki suggests supporting both if possible.
- How much persistence is right? The genre evidence says light meta (cosmetics, no power), while mobile-web norms (daily missions, pass) push toward daily hooks; the user's optional-profile requirement suggests cosmetics-only persistence, which needs validating with playtests.
- What is the one novel physical verb? Unicorn-mounted delivery with a ragdoll rider and a shared giant-glizzy carry are candidates, but none is validated; the AV Club critique implies this is the make-or-break question.
- Story from the user: the genre needs only a premise paragraph, a boss voice and a quota; confirm whether the user wants more narrative than that, because more story is not supported by any hit in the sample.
- Session model: bounded ~30-minute runs (3 shifts) versus infinite drop-in servers; the friendslop evidence favors bounded runs, browser io-games favor infinite lobbies.
- Distribution order: direct link/room code first, then Poki/CrazyGames, then Discord Activities? Each channel imposes technical constraints (bundle size, CSP, safe areas) that are cheaper to meet early.
- Unverified specifics worth a second pass when fetches are possible: PEAK launch price, Content Warning cosmetic DLC, JKLM.fun player caps and monetization, Krunker guest-play details, and the exact Wikipedia wording of the friendslop lead.

## C. Mobile-web multiplayer tech landscape (Sept 2026)

**Researcher summary.** Landscape as of 30 Sep 2026 (versions pulled from the npm registry / GitHub today): Phaser 4.2.1 (4.0.0 shipped 10 Apr 2026 with a rebuilt node-based WebGL renderer), PixiJS 8.21.0, KAPLAY 3001.0.19 stable (v4000 in alpha), Excalibur 0.32.0 (still pre-1.0), three.js r186 (0.186.1), Colyseus 0.18.8, Socket.IO 4.8.4, PlayroomKit 0.0.97, partyserver 0.5.10, Nakama 3.41.0 (18 Sep 2026). Two landscape shifts matter: Hathora (game-server hosting) shut down on 5 May 2026 after the team joined Fireworks AI, so it is off the table; and Vercel Functions gained native WebSocket support in public beta (22 Jun 2026) but connections still die at the function duration limit (300 s default, 800 s max) and are not pinned to the same instance, so Vercel still cannot host a stateful game-room server. Supabase Realtime is a relay with hard message quotas (Free: 100 msg/s total, 200 concurrent connections; Pro: 500 msg/s) and no authoritative logic, so it fits lobbies, presence and turn-based/async play, not 20 Hz action. Supabase Auth gives you guest play via anonymous sign-ins that upgrade in place (user id preserved), but "username + password, no email" is only possible with a synthetic email and email confirmation off, which forfeits Supabase's email-based password reset. On mobile web: iOS still has no element Fullscreen API on iPhone, no ScreenOrientation.lock(), no Vibration API (the checkbox-switch haptics hack was patched in iOS 26.5), closes WebSockets when Safari is backgrounded, throttles rAF to 30 fps in Low Power Mode, and reloads WebGL pages that exceed an undocumented memory ceiling; use svh/dvh units, unlock audio synchronously in a touchend handler, reconnect on visibilitychange, and ship MP3/AAC audio.

Candidate stacks:
1) "Server-authoritative co-op" (recommended for the raccoon PvE action game): Phaser 4 client + Colyseus 0.18 server (self-hosted always-on Fly.io machine ~$10-20/mo, or Colyseus Cloud from $15/mo) + Supabase (anonymous auth, optional username/password upgrade, RLS-protected inventory/outfits tables) + static client on Cloudflare Pages or Vercel. Room codes via Colyseus's custom-room-id recipe (4-letter IDs registered in Presence), built-in reconnection tokens with exponential backoff (0.17+), 20 Hz patch rate by default. Fits real-time PvE and light PvP; extra ops burden vs. a hosted SDK.
2) "Party game, fastest path": Phaser 4 + PlayroomKit + Supabase. PlayroomKit ships room codes, phone-as-controller "stream mode", on-screen joystick, lobbies, RPCs and Discord Activities out of the box, but uses an elected-host client model (no authoritative server), so it fits casual co-op/party and turn-based play, not anti-cheat-sensitive PvP; pricing could not be verified from an official page.
3) "Edge rooms, near-zero idle cost": PixiJS 8 (or Phaser) + partyserver/Durable Objects on Cloudflare + Supabase. One DO per room, WebSocket Hibernation stops duration billing after ~10 s idle, Free plan covers 100k requests/day and 13,000 GB-s/day, incoming WebSocket messages billed 20:1. You write your own tick loop and state diffing (no Colyseus schema), and a DO lives in one location, so trans-continental parties see higher RTT. Best for turn-based/async, lobbies, and small-room PvE at <=20 Hz.

Things I could not confirm as current: PlayroomKit pricing tiers; whether Phaser 4 has any WebGPU path; exact iOS Safari per-tab memory limit; Excalibur 0.32.0 release date (GitHub shows "23 Dec" with no year); Heroic Cloud (Nakama) price points; Colyseus Cloud tiers beyond the "$15/month" entry point; and one March 2026 browser-compat-data issue claiming navigator.vibrate works on iOS Safari (contradicted by every other source, treat as unsupported).

### Principles

1. **Use Phaser 4 (4.2.1 on npm; 4.0.0 released 10 Apr 2026) as the default 2D engine for a mobile-first game: it has a ground-up node-based WebGL renderer, managed WebGL state with context restoration, SpriteGPULayer (a million sprites in one draw call), unified filters/masks, and built-in touch/multi-pointer input. Tradeoff: bigger runtime than PixiJS/KAPLAY, and v4 is only ~5 months old so plugins/tutorials written for v3 may not apply.** (confidence: high)
   - Design implication: Start on Phaser 4.x (not 3.90); keep filters/masks light on phones; budget a ~1-2 MB engine payload; verify any third-party plugin against v4 before depending on it.
   - Why it matters: The renderer rewrite is what makes v4 viable on phones; picking v3 now means porting later.
   - Source: Phaser GitHub releases (v4.0.0 10 Apr 2026, v4.2.1 9 Jul 2026), v4.0.0 release notes, npm registry — https://github.com/phaserjs/phaser/releases/tag/v4.0.0
2. **Phaser's own writeup claims mobile-specific gains in v4 (a filter/mask buffer overhaul reported as up to 16x faster on mobile and ~16 MB less memory). I could only see this in search snippets from phaser.io (site blocked to my fetcher), and the v4.0.0/v4.2.0 GitHub notes do not repeat the 16x figure.** (confidence: medium)
   - Design implication: Treat the 16x number as marketing until you profile on a mid-range Android and an iPhone SE-class device; still, prefer v4's filter system over v3's FX pipeline for any post-processing.
   - Source: phaser.io 'Phaser 3 vs Phaser 4' (May 2026), via search snippet — https://phaser.io/news/2026/05/phaser-3-vs-phaser-4
3. **PixiJS 8.21.0 is the lean renderer alternative (WebGL + WebGPU with Safari WGSL fallback added in 8.20.0; 8.21.0 adds TS 5/6/7 support and renderer loader extensions). Tradeoff: no scenes, physics, tweens, input helpers or audio; you assemble those yourself.** (confidence: high)
   - Design implication: Choose PixiJS if you want to own the game loop and netcode integration tightly (e.g., custom Durable Objects sync); choose Phaser if you want batteries included.
   - Source: npm registry (8.21.0), PixiJS GitHub releases, PixiJS v8.20.0 announcement — https://github.com/pixijs/pixijs/releases
4. **KAPLAY (stable 3001.0.19; v4000 is in alpha, latest 4000.0.0-alpha.27.1 on 12 May 2026) and Excalibur (0.32.0, still 0.x) are fine for jams but carry churn risk for a product: KAPLAY's next major is unreleased and Excalibur has not reached 1.0.** (confidence: high)
   - Design implication: Do not build the production client on KAPLAY v4000 alpha; if you like KAPLAY's API, pin 3001.x. Treat Excalibur as a TypeScript-first option with a smaller ecosystem.
   - Source: npm registry (kaplay 3001.0.19, excalibur 0.32.0), KAPLAY and Excalibur GitHub releases — https://github.com/kaplayjs/kaplay/releases
5. **3D via three.js (r186 / 0.186.1) is viable on mid-range phones only with strict budgets: guidance for mobile web scenes is roughly 10-30k triangles per view, simple (Lambert-style) materials, and treating texture memory (mobile browsers cap WebGL memory around 128-256 MB) as the real bottleneck. WebGPU now ships in Safari 26 and Chrome Android, but you still need a WebGL2 fallback.** (confidence: low)
   - Design implication: For 'riding unicorns' with a cartoon look, 2D sprites (or pre-rendered 3D to sprite sheets) will hit 60 fps on far more phones than live 3D; if you go 3D, use low-poly, few draw calls, KTX2/Basis compressed textures and a WebGL2 fallback.
   - Source: npm registry (three 0.186.1); VIVERSE mobile optimization guide (search snippet); Safari 26 WebGPU coverage (search snippet) — https://docs.viverse.com/optimization/challenges-and-optimization-strategies
6. **Colyseus 0.18 (0.18.8 on npm; 0.18.x core packages released this week) is the most complete open-source room server for browser games: server-authoritative state with property-level binary patches, joinOrCreate/joinById/create, private/locked/unlisted rooms, LobbyRoom and QueueRoom, transports for WebSocket (default), uWebSockets.js, Bun and experimental WebTransport, and hosting on Colyseus Cloud or self-host (Docker/PM2). 0.17 added automatic SDK reconnection with exponential backoff.** (confidence: high)
   - Design implication: Use Colyseus for the authoritative PvE loop (raccoon AI, loot rolls, damage) so clients cannot cheat inventory; keep only cosmetic/UI state client-side.
   - Source: Colyseus docs (llms.txt in colyseus/docs), Colyseus GitHub releases, npm registry — https://github.com/colyseus/colyseus/releases
7. **Colyseus room codes: the official 'Custom Room ID' recipe generates a short (e.g., 4-letter) code in onCreate, registers it with presence.sadd(), checks collisions with presence.smembers(), removes it in onDispose() with presence.srem(), and clients join with joinById(code). Mark the room private so joinOrCreate/matchmaking ignore it.** (confidence: high)
   - Design implication: Implement 'Create party' = client.create('party') returning roomId as the code; 'Join party' = client.joinById(CODE). Use an unambiguous alphabet (no 0/O, 1/I) and uppercase-normalize input.
   - Source: Colyseus docs recipe custom-room-id.mdx (raw from colyseus/docs on GitHub); canonical page docs.colyseus.io/recipes — https://docs.colyseus.io/recipes
8. **Colyseus reconnection for phones: call allowReconnection(client, seconds | 'manual') inside onDrop() to hold the seat; the SDK retries with exponential backoff for transient drops (room instance survives), firing onDrop/onReconnect or onLeave with code 4003; for page reloads or app restarts the client must persist room.reconnectionToken (e.g., sessionStorage) and call client.reconnect(token).** (confidence: high)
   - Design implication: Persist the reconnection token after join and after every onReconnect; on visibilitychange->visible, attempt reconnect before showing 'You were disconnected'; hold seats ~30-60 s in PvE.
   - Source: Colyseus docs room/reconnection.mdx (raw from colyseus/docs) — https://github.com/colyseus/docs/blob/master/pages/room/reconnection.mdx

### Pitfalls

- Hosting the game room on Vercel: WebSockets now work there (beta, Jun 2026) but connections die at 300-800 s and are not sticky, so match state cannot live in memory.
- Using Supabase Realtime Broadcast/Presence as the movement channel: Free plan is 100 msg/s project-wide and Presence caps each client at 5 updates per 30 s; one 20 Hz room exhausts it.
- Planning on Hathora: hosting shut down 5 May 2026.
- Assuming Android behaviors on iPhone: no element Fullscreen, no orientation lock, no navigator.vibrate, WebSocket closed on app switch, rAF capped at 30 fps in Low Power Mode, and page reloads on WebGL memory pressure.
- Starting AudioContext on load or inside a promise callback; iOS needs a synchronous start inside a real touch handler.
- Using 100vh for the canvas container; use 100svh/100dvh and re-fit on resize.
- Trusting a host-elected client (PlayroomKit) for inventory or currency: any persistent reward must be validated server-side or accepted as inflatable.
- Building on KAPLAY v4000 alpha or on Phaser 3 plugins with Phaser 4; both invite rewrites.
- Username-only accounts with a synthetic email: there is no built-in password recovery; make that explicit and offer optional email linking or recovery codes.
- 4096x4096 RGBA atlases and OGG Vorbis audio: the first blows iOS memory budgets, the second never plays in Safari.
- One Durable Object per room without locationHint: far-away friends get the full trans-continental RTT; also count incoming WebSocket messages (20:1 billing) when estimating cost.
- Sending positions instead of inputs, or rendering remote players at the latest snapshot without an interpolation buffer, produces rubber-banding even on good networks.

### Pitfalls

- Gun double meaning: any shooting mechanic, 'clip', 'extended', or blaster imagery re-activates the Glock sense of 'glizzy' and can push the rating up or draw store-review scrutiny; a live 'GLIZZY GUN' toy trademark filing (SN 98464275) also sits in that lane.
- Sexual innuendo: 'glizzy gobbler' content on TikTok is where the word gets NSFW; suggestive eating animations, 'gobbler' achievements, or deep-throat jokes will get flagged by parents and can trip app-store screenshot review.
- Slang saturation: Sheetz, Oscar Mayer, and food press have all used 'glizzy' in headlines by mid-2026, which usually means the originating youth cohort has moved on; a game whose only joke is the word will age fast.
- Generic single-word namespace: 'Glizzy' alone is used by at least five small indie games (Dungeons of Glizzy, GLIZZY horror, Glizzy Gobblers, Glizzy Gobbler: Reheated, Glizzy Bird) and many music handles; always brand as the full two-word name with a distinctive mascot.
- Trademark not formally cleared: only Justia search snippets were seen (GLIZZY GVNG clothing, GLIZZY GUN toys); no USPTO TESS search or attorney clearance was performed and the Justia pages could not be opened.
- Domain/handle availability is unverified: RDAP/WHOIS and direct site checks were blocked by network egress; 'no search results' is not proof the .com is free.
- Competitor scan incomplete: searches for raccoon-as-enemy games and unicorn-riding games (e.g., to differentiate from Robot Unicorn Attack-style runners) did not run because the session's web-search budget was exhausted and page fetches are blocked; do not treat those design pillars as validated as unique.
